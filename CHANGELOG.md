## [Unreleased]

### Added

**The Nexus – the system the game is named after, specified**
- **The Nexus is Dardan's Lord-Kit system, four abilities that all run over the band between him and his allies** – *Exchange*, *Lifeline*, *Heartpulse*, *Earthbound* – and only Dardan has it. No enemy version exists as an ability; the enemy's Nexus is the Dajjal's chain, a boss mechanic of Level 52. Nexus abilities target the player's own units only; *Earthbound* is the one written exception. Summoned Solmare beasts are excluded from all four, closing the question Beast Summon left open
- **Three of the four scale with the support rank between Dardan and the unit** (Exchange range, Lifeline range, Heartpulse strength). A bond *is* the Support System's rank and the Nexus reads it – which answers the "Trust & Bonds: separate system?" question with no. The rank scale does not exist yet, so the per-rank tables in the Balancing Guide are empty by dependency; two requirements are recorded for the Support System: the eight Vigilant Knights start above rank zero with Dardan, and Elena's rank in Ch 08 is high enough for the Exchange – she is a mother to them
- **The band is a state: golden (available) or black (locked for the chapter), set by the chapter's level box.** The rule lives in the spec; which chapters are black is a **pending story revision, not a spec gap** – the chapters were written before the band had a state and Dardan will revise them. What they show today (Ch 40A ends grey, Ch 41–43 black, Ch 44 golden again, Ch 30's box already switches Exchange off) is recorded in the spec as the note for that revision
- **Elena is a player-faction unit on Level 08 only – forced deployment, one map, heal-only.** The Ch 08 tutorial needs the Exchange to reach her, she joins the roster only in Ch 25, and the "Nexus targets own units only" rule was not going to bend for one map. Her battle-local data for that map is Level 08's
- **Exchange costs a flat global turn cooldown, not MP and not uses per map.** MP would tie the signature move to Mag, which Dardan's sword line never grows; uses-per-map get hoarded for the boss; a cooldown makes every swap a timing decision, and the inherent price is already in the form – Dardan ends where the ally stood and cannot move away. The rank scales the range, nothing scales the cooldown: one lever. Range goes through enemies, walls and barriers (Ch 05); both landing tiles must be legal for the unit that lands there; the ally's status is irrelevant. **Available from Ch 08, not Ch 05** – Level 05 must not offer it, because Dardan is meant to be trapped inside the barrier; Ch 08's hostage is the tutorial
- **Lifeline can kill Dardan – there is no 1-HP floor.** A share of every attack on the linked ally is taken from Dardan's HP instead, at any distance, until he lifts it. A burden that cannot kill him is not a decision. **Status effects are not shared – they go to Dardan whole:** poison, sleep, any debuff the linked ally would receive lands on Dardan alone, so a linked healer cannot be silenced but a sleep arrow aimed at her switches the whole kit off for a turn. A status Dardan is immune to fizzles (conservative, open). Introduction chapter still open – Dardan is deciding
- **Nexus Mastery moves from Lv 45 to Part 08 (Ch 49–52) and grows:** it removes **every** Nexus cooldown – Exchange and Earthbound – and lengthens the Exchange and Lifeline ranges at every support rank (value in the Balancing Guide, not yet set). The Tower is where the band is never on cooldown and reaches furthest, which is what the Dajjal fight is built on. Whether the trigger is a chapter or a level is open; Bond of Souls (Lv 54) is the kit's other Part 08 unlock. The Progression System's ability timeline row at Lv 45 is corrected minimally
- **Sabotage (Saboteur, Advanced) is replaced by *Disarray*.** Sabotage disabled an enemy's arts, magic and abilities at range 2 for any Saboteur from Ch 25 – an Advanced class that is not even a Master class held a stronger disable than the Lord's own signature seal. Disarray keeps the class's identity (wrecking what the enemy relies on) and takes what was left after Unmaking (weapons) and Cutpurse (items): the **formation** – every enemy adjacent to a chosen enemy within range 2 is pushed one tile away from it, Strength-gated like Shove, no damage. Hold Fast, Shield Brother, Pit Fighter, Guard and the healer behind the wall lose their neighbour. Not a seal, not a hold, and the Earthbound overlap is gone
- **Heartpulse is once per chapter, the one place in the kit where uses-per-map is right:** Ch 21 fixed it, and a map-wide heal without a limit would replace every healer. It reaches every allied unit on the map and heals each by that unit's own rank with Dardan
- **Earthbound seals one enemy's access to the band – abilities, combat arts, tomes, staves and its auras – for N rounds; the plain weapon strike remains.** It closes, it never controls, per the Guru's "dem Band vertrauen statt es zu kontrollieren"; it is not a hold and not Geo magic. Full action, fixed range (proposed 1–2, no rank exists with an enemy), no hit roll, turn cooldown rather than once-per-map so that it is a tool on every map from Ch 44, not a boss-killer saved for eight chapters. **The Dajjal holds his units on a chain: every chained unit heals him at once and the reinforcements never stop, so killing them is a treadmill – Earthbound cuts the chain for its duration and opens the only window.** The numbers of that fight are Level 52's. The name is a nod to *EarthBound* (Mother 2), and only its principle is taken: the final boss falls to what the player built over the whole game, not to damage
- **Nexus abilities take no Capacity and cannot be unequipped** – Exchange arrives at Ch 08 when Dardan is in a Base class with Capacity 3 and three Citizen abilities already competing for it
- **The level index moves *The Nexus / Exchange* from Ch 07 to Ch 08**; Ch 07 carries what the Progression System already said – the first chapter in the chosen classes. Ch 21 (Heartpulse) and Ch 44 (Earthbound) are annotated
- Balancing Guide gains a *The Nexus* section: cooldowns, Lifeline share, Earthbound range/duration, and the three per-rank tables with rank rows waiting for the Support System. Proposed: Heartpulse heals a percentage of max HP per rank (it cannot read Mag and must stay meaningful from Ch 21 to Ch 52), staying below a staff heal on any one target; Earthbound cooldown at or above its duration, so one enemy is sealed at a time

**Tooling**
- `.claude/settings.json` – project settings, committed. An allowlist for read-only shell commands (ls, grep, wc, git status/log/diff/show …) removes the confirmation prompts for inspection work, and a `PreToolUse` hook on `git commit` runs the invariant checks
- `.claude/hooks/pre-commit-checks.sh` – **mechanical enforcement of the three countable invariants**, which until now depended on someone remembering them: a surviving `{{PLACEHOLDER}}` blocks the commit; a new file in a content folder with no README link warns; touching `design/` or `story/` without `CHANGELOG.md` warns. Skills judge, hooks count – a hook cannot tell whether a Speech Pattern is structure or attitude, but it never forgets to look. Two exemptions the placeholder check needs to be usable: `_TEMPLATE.md` files are skipped, since holding placeholders is what a template is for, and text inside backticks is stripped before scanning, because skills and this changelog talk *about* placeholders constantly
- `.githooks/pre-commit` – **the same checks on every commit, not only Claude's.** The Claude Code hook only fires on commits Claude makes; a placeholder check is most useful when *anyone* fills in a template, so the script runs in two modes (JSON for the agent, text plus exit 1 for git) and `core.hooksPath` points at a versioned directory. Enable after a fresh clone with `git config core.hooksPath .githooks`; bypass a single commit with `git commit --no-verify`
- **Every file states in its first lines what it is and what it governs.** Seven of nine catalog files opened straight into a table, which is how `Magic-Tomes.md` came to be read as a list of learnable spells instead of a weapon catalog. The lore files got the same treatment
- **An empty or half-filled table always means "not filled in yet", never "deliberately nothing".** Recorded as an invariant so no session reads a gap as a statement or fills it with invented values

**Calendar**
- **Imperial years convert to real years with a fixed offset of 660** – anchored on Imperial Year 1337 = 1997, chosen because 1337 is *leet* and 1997 is the author's birth year. Day and month are identical; only the year shifts. Game start is Imperial Year 1357, i.e. 2017, which makes a character's age derivable rather than free: `age = 1357 − imperial year of birth`. Written into `Calendar.md` with the conversion steps and into `charactercraft` as a check
- The twelve month names in `Calendar.md` are declared complete – there is no thirteenth month and no alternative naming
- **All character sheets brought in line with the calendar.** Applying the rule surfaced eleven sheets that broke it, in two systematic clusters rather than as isolated slips: three sheets (Aldric, Kassandra, Rohan) computed ages from a start year of 1347, two (Hasan, Asta) from 1358. Ages were kept and birth years moved, except for Asta, who is Shira's age. **Hasan is now born in 1337 like Dardan** – his backstory has the two of them "both six years old", which does not work with a year between them. Six invented month names replaced with the calendar months implied by each real-world date, and three sheets had the imperial year sitting in the real-world *Birthday* field

**Working rules**
- Character sheets are now governed by two skills instead of none: `charactercraft` for the narrative half (person, appearance, personality, backstory, recruitment framing, supports, story role) and `statcraft` for the stat block (joining level, base stats, growth rates, max stats, class path, abilities, weapon proficiencies)
- **A character sheet has two owners.** Lorekeeper writes the prose half, Rulewright derives the numbers – neither touches the other's sections. Splitting the sheet keeps stat blocks out of narrative rewrites and keeps invented classes out of the catalog
- `statcraft` defines the derivation order for a unit – joining level → class path → archetype → base stats → growth budget → max stats – and a reachability check (`base + growth × remaining level-ups + class modifiers`), so a max stat can no longer be a number no unit can reach
- Arbiter and Rulewright can load skills (`Skill` added to their toolsets); Arbiter is instructed to treat them as a floor, not as the full scope of a review
- **The two halves of a character sheet have a fixed order: narrative first, numbers second.** A unit's personal ability is the mechanical translation of what the prose half says about the person, so deriving stats first means guessing the character. Recorded in the routing table, `charactercraft` and `statcraft`
- `mechanicraft` skill – binding rules for specifying systems in `design/mechanics/`: purpose in one sentence, the five-pillar run written out rather than ticked off, cost, interaction, acquisition, and the field that is missing most often – **which chapter introduces the system, and how the player learns it without a tutorial popup**
- **The line that decides where a number lives: does changing it change the *system* or only its *strength*?** "Resonance occurs on Fibonacci rounds" is a rule and stays in the mechanic document; "resonance grants +15 % hit" is a tuning value and belongs in the Balancing Guide
- **Three new mandatory sections in the mechanics template: `Kosten`, `Design Pillars`, `Einführung`.** The template described what a system does and never why it exists or what it costs. Cost was buried in *Kernregeln*, the pillar run lived only in the author's head, and the introduction chapter existed solely as a column in the levels index pointing one way. The five existing mechanic files predate them and are filled in when next touched – a pillar run is a judgement and an introduction chapter is a decision, so neither can be back-filled mechanically
- **The catalog is the registry; three places link to it.** A mechanic document says *what* its entries are, the catalog lists *which* exist, and the character sheet and `Unit-Classes.md` reference the same list. The mechanics template's `Listen & Katalog` section is replaced by a mandatory `Katalog` section holding exactly one link – it was the cause of both known duplicates. Resolving them migrated the content that existed only in the mechanic file first: *Adept* and *Vantage* into `catalog/Abilities.md`, the **Element** column into `catalog/Combat-Arts.md`
- `notes/Mechanics-Drift.md` – dated inventory of `design/mechanics/`. Open findings: **The Nexus, the ability the game is named after, has no specification**, along with six further High-priority systems; `mechanics/Abilities.md` and `mechanics/Combat-Arts.md` each duplicate their catalog counterpart and have already drifted apart (one by content, one by schema); `Biorythm.md` holds biorhythm types for 14 characters in a systems document while no sheet has a field for it
- `levelcraft` skill – binding rules for level design in `design/levels/`: derivation order (what the scene demands → objective → carrying mechanic → map → enemy roster → boss → reinforcements → rewards → difficulty variants), the deployment and enemy-level bands, and the pillar check applied to a battle
- **A level has two owners, like a character sheet.** The short level box lives in the chapter text and belongs to Lorekeeper; the design document in `design/levels/` belongs to Rulewright. Both describe the same battle, so `levelcraft` makes their agreement a mandatory check – and where they disagree, the chapter wins, because it is written, read and anchored in the story
- **At most one new mechanic per level, and the map must carry it.** A level whose new mechanic can be ignored has not introduced it
- `notes/Level-Design-Drift.md` – dated inventory of the level documentation. Open findings, not yet decided: Part 05 deploys 8 units per chapter while Part 06 deploys 11, against a documented limit of 8-10 for the same chapter range and two strands the spec requires to be equivalent; the level box format in `Writing-Style-Guide.md` describes an English header that none of the 48 existing boxes use
- `notes/Character-Sheet-Drift.md` – dated inventory of where existing sheets deviate from the two skills, with file, finding and severity. The skills previously carried these counts inline; a binding rules document that states "26 of 28 sheets are empty" becomes wrong the moment someone fills one, and is then read as a rule

**Character sheet template**
- `story/characters/_TEMPLATE.md` rewritten to define **structure only**. It previously carried design decisions as mandatory fields and most of them were stale: a class path with the discarded tier levels (Citizen 1 → Base 5 → … → Unique 35), "max stats at level 45", a growth budget of 300-420 % measured over nine columns including MP, archetype target percentages in the comments, an *Affinity* column that exists nowhere in `design/`, and *Magic* as a weapon type. Numbers belong to the Balancing Guide and rules to the skills; the template now holds neither
- **Every named character gets a stat block**, replacing "unplayable means no stats" – most story characters are bosses, and a boss without stats cannot be built. Three kinds of unit: playable units get the full derivation (base, growth, max); named non-playable units get *Stats by Appearance*, one row per chapter they appear in and no growth curve, because a boss never levels; generic enemies get no sheet at all and are described entirely in the level document with the stats of that one battle
- **Stats for a named unit live on its sheet, never also in the level document.** The level template's boss stat table is replaced by a link plus the battle-local data (equipment, AI pattern, position, dialogue)
- Dropped from the sheet: the H1 title (the filename and *Personal Information* already carry the name; no existing sheet had one), the *Verwandte Dateien* line and the *Related Content* list, which collected the same links twice

**Repository structure**
- Split the documentation by lifecycle into three top-level folders: `design/` (normative spec, English), `story/` (narrative content, German), `notes/` (dated analysis, not normative)
- Folder index pages as `README.md`, so GitHub renders each folder's index as its front page

**Progression design**
- Level curve across all 8 parts, 1 → 60, cap reached at the Dajjal (Ch 52)
- Promotion gates: base class at end of Ch 06 (story event), Intermediate Lv 15, Advanced Lv 30, Master/Unique Lv 45
- Recruitment level per part – only the eight Vigilant Knights start at Level 1 in the Citizen class
- Epilogue definition: Ch 53-56 grant no XP; support ranks max out there instead
- Catch-up XP rule for units below their group's average level
- Rule that Part 05 and Part 06 must share identical enemy level bands, which makes the split roster self-balancing
- **MP** documented as a stat in the Balancing Guide (base values, growth rates, cap), budgeted separately from the eight combat stats
- Backlog table in Game Mechanics listing undocumented systems by priority, replacing links to files that were never written

**Weapon, tome and class catalogs – structure pass**
- **Weapon ranks run F–S and the cap rises with class tier; S opens only at Master.** A hybrid class's secondary weapon types cap one step below its main type, so breadth costs depth and the single-type branch at the same fork is never strictly worse. Rank F exists only in the Citizen class – the eight Vigilant Knights are the only units that ever hold it. Every weapon and tome table gained a `Rank` column (empty); the caps per tier are a proposal table in the Balancing Guide
- **Staff is a weapon type, not an ability.** Healers wield a Mag-based, rank-gated staff with Uses like any other weapon; it stands outside both triangles. An Acolyte cannot attack until promotion at Lv 15 – that is by design. Healing with a staff grants XP (value: the existing heal entry in the XP formula)
- **Battle Staff carries Guard:** melee attacks (range 1) against the wielder suffer a Hit penalty, ranged attacks ignore it – a staff parries what it sees coming. Value proposed in the Balancing Guide, below the triangle swing so Guard never flips a matchup by itself
- **Artillery has a minimum range of 2 or more**, expressed only through the Range column; the same convention applies to siege tomes
- **`Move Type` column (Infantry, Cavalry, Flying, Armored) on every class table**, filled from the class names – it is the key that effective weapons and the still-unwritten Movement rules refer to
- **Elementalist rule:** the second Natura element must be one the first element loses to – the player closes his weakness instead of stacking advantages. The Arcanist's third element is free
- **Three Natura edges added – Geo > Pyro (earth and sand smother the fire), Cryo > Geo (frost wedging: ice in the cracks breaks the rock), Electro > Aero (the lightning rules the storm) – for twelve in total.** The Elementalist rule (second element = one the first loses to) is only a choice if an element has two weaknesses; with nine edges five of seven elements had one. Seven further candidates were rejected for lacking a physical reason a sceptical reader would accept (Aero > Pyro, Cryo > Aero, Electro > Cryo, Hydro > Geo, Dendro > Electro, Cryo > Electro, Aero > Cryo). **Electro and Cryo deliberately keep a single weakness** – their Elementalist's second element is forced (Geo, Pyro) and that is the price of their extra win. Resulting profiles, stated as design: Geo duelist (3/2), Pyro balanced (2/2), Electro and Cryo offensive (2/1), Aero/Hydro/Dendro support and control (1/2) whose value lies in reactions and utility. Tome tuning compensates the 1/2 profile – a proposal in the Balancing Guide. Every edge now carries a one-sentence physical reason in `Magic-Tomes.md`
- **Weapon Rank Progression** added to the mechanics backlog – how a rank rises (weapon XP through use, the Fire Emblem convention) is a decision not yet taken; the scale and the caps exist, the climb does not
- **The weapon catalog is filled from Bronze to Silver – 73 entries across ten types.** Tier and rank are two axes, not one: Bronze F, Iron E, Steel D, Silver A, Legendary S leaves ranks C and B empty of tiers, so the variants live there (Effective and magic weapons at C, Killer and the Steel-grade reach weapons at B, Brave alongside Silver at A). Every promotion therefore hands the player a new *kind* of weapon rather than a bigger number, and rank is both gate and price
- **Bronze, a new tier below Iron** – 0.6× Might, +5 Hit, **Critical 0**, 15 Uses, 0.4× cost. Rank F exists for six chapters before anyone chooses a base class, so it needed a weapon at all. Worse in the stat that decides a kill, better in the stat that teaches the game; no critical, because a player still learning the damage maths should not have it overturned by a random roll in either direction. Bronze is meant to be replaced around Ch 09, not maintained
- **The Gauntlet double is a property of the type, not a variant** ("Flurry"): every gauntlet strikes twice on both phases, Defense is subtracted from each strike, and the Brave rule's −10 Hit is already priced into every row. Gauntlets therefore get no Brave variant. The counterpart is the Crushing Gauntlet, which gives Flurry up for double Might – identical against 0 Defense, far better against armour, and a genuine two-way choice instead of an upgrade
- **Staves are filled as a usable line** (Heal E → Mend D → Physic C → Recover B → Restore B → Rescue A → Fortify A → Warp S). **There is deliberately no revival staff** – loss is permanent, and a staff that undid it would undo the weight every other rule is built to carry
- **57 magic tomes, named by intensity** (Ember → Flame → Inferno, Spark → Bolt → Thunder, Shade → Gloom → Abyss …). Each element gets a three-step damage line, one or two utility tomes expressing its role from the Magic System, and exactly one siege tome at rank A. **Siege tomes share their own naming register – the natural disaster** (Firestorm, Blizzard, Earthquake, Nightfall) – so the one tome that cannot defend its wielder is recognisable by name alone. Umbra's HP drain is written as the element's signature: it is the only element that gives HP back, and it pays for it in Might
- **Tomes cost MP and have no Uses**, so MP is their real price: E 3 · C 5 · A 7 · siege 14. Read against the regeneration table, a mid-game mage fires a C tome every round and a siege tome roughly every second round – the artillery cadence, stated rather than left to emerge
- **Magic Effect Values in the Balancing Guide** – one shared Rank Bonus scale (E +6 … S +21) behind healing, shields, buffs, debuffs, poison, regeneration and drain, so each value exists exactly once. **Healing is `Mag + Rank Bonus`**, deliberately calibrated to stay worth about half a unit's max HP across all 52 progression chapters: one heal undoes one bad exchange, never two. A flat value would make healers irrelevant by Part 04, pure Mag scaling would make them irrelevant in Part 01. The number lives on the rank because a staff has no Might – which also makes every promotion a healer takes visible the next time it heals. **Poison can never take a unit below 1 HP**: under permadeath, a death three turns after the causing decision is a death the player could not see coming
- **Every class has one Class Ability on entry and one Mastery Ability learned later** – 71 class and 85 mastery abilities catalogued, with combat arts to match. Where the tree gives a class no weapon-type advantage, the ability is the only differentiator: the four Paladins are told apart by their mastery, the nine Sages by their element's role, the four fliers by what each is effective against
- **Combat arts apply elements in two layers, predictable from a class's weapon types alone:** the primary element is the physics of the strike (Sword→Aero, Lance→Cryo, Axe→Geo, Knife→Umbra, Gauntlet→Electro, Chain and Battle Staff→Hydro, Bow→Dendro, Artillery→Pyro), the secondary is the school that wields it, at double MP. The mapping was chosen so most hybrid classes reach two elements that react with each other – which is what they get in exchange for capping one weapon rank lower on their second type

**Chain classes**
- **Two Chain-primary class lines: Tamer → Dompteur → Bestiarius → Behemoth (Lord Title Legiana) and Tamer → Harpooner → Retiarius → Leviathan (Lord Title Lagiacrus), all Infantry.** The Chain was the only weapon type with no class of its own – its pulls lived on a Gladiator side-ability and its Silver entry was reachable only at Master. Tamer is a Base class chosen at the end of Ch 06 like every other; the Citizen already holds Chain at F from Ch 04. Chain is the main type of every class on both lines; the Harpooner line adds the Lance as a secondary type under the existing one-rank-lower rule. Growth profile for the whole line: Str main, Dex and Spd supports, one row in the Balancing Guide's per-line table. The pure line is the tamer – anti-Flying, movement restriction, displacement, the Snare Chain as its home weapon (Leash, Bring Down, Beast Call, Hurl, Apex Predator, Snatch). The Harpooner line is the sailor – a fully ranged close-combat unit whose Lances throw as well as its Chains, anti-Cavalry through the Lance (Harpoon, Receive Charge, Cast Net, Breakwater, Wake, Deep Water). Class combat arts follow the catalog convention: Lash Down and Net and Trident at Advanced, Talon Throw and Harpoon Line at Master, on the Chain's Hydro/Electro and the Lance's Cryo/Geo
- **Bestiarius' class ability is a summon – Beast Call – and only its row exists so far.** It spends MP to place a Solmare beast (the beasts of Ch 22/23) as an allied AI-controlled unit that takes the Bestiarius' orders, three per map, no XP either way, counts for no objective, stays until it falls. The MP cost is marked not yet set in the Balancing Guide; the full rules and the beast's stats are a separate mechanic document still to be written. The Behemoth's Apex Predator already builds on "beasts summoned by this unit"
- **`mechanics/Beast-Summon.md` – the beast is a unit, not an effect.** The Bestiarius needed something a pull or a push could not be: a body on the map that fights on its own. The Solmare beast is an Other-faction unit (the yellow units the player knows from Rohan in Ch 22) with a stat block **derived from its summoner at the moment of the call** – multipliers on the tamer's own stats, no level, no growth, no Mag, no MP, a natural weapon outside both triangles – so a stronger tamer fields a stronger beast and nothing has to be tuned per chapter. Three summons per Bestiarius per map, never refunded, action plus MP each. The summoner steers it with five orders – Halt, Roam, Go to tile, Follow, Attack target – but **only within the reach of its Chain**: the leash is the rule that gives the summon its shape, a tamer who wants control stays with the animal and one who sends it away accepts that it runs on its own AI. The beast gives and earns no XP, counts for no objective and never causes a defeat, so spending it is a decision about the summon, not about the map. It gets nothing a rule did not explicitly give it – no staves, no auras, no Exchange, no chain attack – written in the conservative form with ten open decisions listed for Dardan. The wild beasts of Ch 22/23 are declared the same unit at enemy scaling, left to `levelcraft` when those levels are designed. Reference named by Dardan: Tanith's *Reinforce* in *Path of Radiance* – only "summoned allies act on their own" is taken from it. Tuning values sit in a new *Beast Summon* section of the Balancing Guide, all not yet set

**Puppeteer**
- **Puppeteer – the Master class of the Trickster line (Knife, Chain), Lord Title Whisperer of Varnel, and the only class that lays traps.** Dardan reserved traps for this line; they are written at ability level, without a new mechanic document. *Tripwire* (class ability) spends the action to lay a wire on an empty tile within range 2 – the first enemy that moves onto or through it stops there, its move ends and its action for the turn is lost; the wire is spent when it fires, one per Puppeteer at a time, unfired wires last the map. *Pull the Strings* (mastery) lets the Puppeteer keep several wires (count → Balancing Guide, not yet set) and drags an enemy caught within range 2 of it to the adjacent tile before it loses its action – a pull on a trap, paid in advance and fired once, which is why it is an ability and the "pulling on hit is a combat art" rule stands. **Wires are hidden from the enemy AI but always visible to the player, his own and the enemy's** – a trap the player cannot see would be hidden information; the asymmetry is human versus machine, not side versus side. The Puppeteer needs no new art: *Unmaking* moves up to it from the Saboteur

**Growth model and the Citizen**
- **Growth is personal growth plus a class growth modifier, and the modifier scales with tier** (`mechanics/Growth-Modifiers.md`). With purely personal growths a Knight with a physical profile sent into a mage class at Ch 06 was a trap, and the Progression System forbids trap branches under permanent promotion. Every class now carries a per-stat modifier added on each level-up spent in it – Base < Intermediate < Advanced < Master, Citizen 0 – so the class choice is visible over 59 level-ups, not only as a one-time stat bonus at the promotion screen. Values are a proposal in the Balancing Guide, grouped by class line (main stat + two supports, +5 per tier, branch overrides for Flying, Armored, Cleric and Martial Monk), never negative, and derived from one guard: **no Base class may leave a Knight's main attack stat below the Medium band even from Low personal growth** (20 + 20 ≥ 40). Str and Mag stay two separate growths because Mag feeds MP income for every unit. The `statcraft` reachability check must now run segment by segment along the canon class path – a sheet's Max Stats are canon-path values; the skill file is to be updated from the spec
- **Aptitude is a flat +X % to every growth rate, X proposed at 10.** The flat-bonus form follows the *Awakening* / *Three Houses* precedent; the size is halved against it because it stacks on the class modifier under a cap of 50 with no reclassing – at +20 a High-growth Knight would cap his main stat in Part 05/06. At +10 the eight Knights' effective budget reads 380–480 % (personal + 80), about six points per stat over 59 level-ups: one promotion's worth ahead of a same-class veteran, which is the intended size of "the orphans overtake the veterans"
- **A Citizen's MP regime follows the equipped weapon:** tome → mage rules (MP per attack, passive regeneration, element on every attack), physical weapon or staff → non-mage rules. Adaptability lets it flip mid-turn; proposed as acceptable without a guard (once per turn, the smallest values on both tables, no Combat Arts to spend it on), with a one-sentence fallback guard recorded in the spec for Dardan to decide
- **Elena Flavia is a healer and priestess (Acolyte → Priest), and she taught Leona and Lina staff healing and the basics of Lux magic in the orphanage.** This is the in-world reason the Citizens can hold a staff from Ch 01 and recognise the tomes in Ch 05 – without it, eight orphans wielding magic had no anchor. Recorded in the three sheets; where Elena learned it herself and her path beyond Priest are marked open, not invented
- **Ch 04 shows the weapon delivery, Ch 05 the tome find.** Ivan unpacks the crates in the new base (knives, gauntlets, chains, battle staves, a field gun) so the staged weapon access is seen rather than announced; in the treasure chamber Lina finds the nine tomes before the Guardian wakes, so the player can cast in Level 05. Nothing explains why the tomes are in the temple – deliberately withheld

### Changed
- **The Thief line forks at Base, not at Intermediate.** Thief now promotes to Rogue *or* Trickster; Rogue promotes to Assassin only. Trickster drops to Intermediate and Saboteur to Advanced, each keeping its two abilities (Sleight of Hand and Misdirection at Capacity 2 fit the Intermediate convention, Sabotage and Cut the Strings at 3 fit Advanced – no values changed); their arts shift one tier because Intermediate classes carry none – Cutpurse becomes the Saboteur's Advanced art, Unmaking the Puppeteer's Master art. Why: the Trickster/Saboteur line is its own identity – Chain, sabotage, traps – and under Rogue it was a dead-end side fork with no Master class of its own. Now Rogue → Assassin → Nightveil is the pure knife line and Trickster → Saboteur → Puppeteer the control line, and the fork is taken at the Intermediate promotion (Lv 15, Ch 12) like every other line's first fork. Consequence accepted by Dardan: Sleight of Hand (Steal may take the equipped weapon) arrives at Intermediate, Ch 12
- **Ensnare is the Tamer's positional Base mastery, not the Gladiator's class ability.** It becomes a Capacity-1 command like Shove: pull a unit within range 2 – ally or enemy – adjacent, failing against an enemy whose Strength is above the user's. Rule now in force: **pulling on hit is a combat art, never an ability** – Riptide Lash and Net Cast remain the only on-hit pulls, and no class gets a pull ability. A pull that fires on every hit with no cost was the one displacement in the catalog with neither a roll, an action nor MP behind it
- **The Gladiator's class ability is Pit Fighter** – while adjacent to two or more enemies its attacks cannot be countered, the mirror of the Duelist's Duel at the same tier: one fights best alone, the other in the press. Weapon types, Disarm, Net Cast and the promotion to Spartan are unchanged
- **Chain is no longer an always-secondary weapon type.** Only the Battle Staff keeps that status. `Weapons.md` now names the Tamer lines among the Chain's carriers, and the Silver Chain reads as an Advanced weapon for them and Master-tier only for the hybrids
- **Shove and Knockback work on enemies, gated by Strength.** Every other enemy displacement in the catalog (Updraft, Trample, Ensnare, the Chain arts) is tied to a hit or costs MP; a Capacity-1 action that pushes an enemy off a chokepoint with no roll and no counter needed a price. It fails against an enemy whose Strength is above the user's – the same idiom Steal uses with Speed – so pushing archers stays a tactic and pushing knights a gamble, with Unbreakable as the hard stop on top
- **Canto's Capacity corrected from 10 to 3.** The ability catalog runs on a 1–4 capacity scale; a 10 was a leftover from a different scale and made the game's most common mounted ability unaffordable to read
- **Vantage moved from General to Class Abilities (Myrmidon).** The Progression System has Dardan learning it at Lv 15 in Myrmidon, which makes it a class ability; leaving it in the scroll pool as well would have been two truths about the same name
