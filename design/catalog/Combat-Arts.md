# Combat Arts

Catalog of every combat art – the active weapon techniques that cost MP and are the only way a non-mage applies an element at all. The rules behind them are in [Game Mechanics](../mechanics/Combat-Arts.md); **which** arts exist is here.

This list is referenced by the character sheet (*Combat Arts*) and by the classes in [Unit Classes](Unit-Classes.md).

**Element** is the point of the table. A mage applies an element with every attack; everyone else applies one only by spending an art. The element an art carries is therefore what decides whether a physical unit can take part in an [elemental reaction](../mechanics/Magic-System.md#elementarreaktionen) at all, and which one.

**MP Cost** is paid out of MP the unit built up by attacking: a non-mage gains +1 to +3 MP per normal attack depending on Mag (see [Magic System](../mechanics/Magic-System.md#nicht-magier--aktiv)). Costs are set so an art is something a unit fights *toward* – two or three attacks for a basic art, four or five for a class signature, most of a Master unit's built-up pool for a terminal one. An art that could be fired every turn would remove the decision the system exists for.

**When arts arrive.** Weapon Combat Arts open at Intermediate, matching the chapter where the player meets combat arts – Ch 19, *Thysian Combat Arts*, in the [Progression System](../Progression-System.md#part-03-the-champions-road-chapters-17-24). Class Combat Arts are taught at Advanced and again at Master. The Base tier has none – a Base unit is still learning where to stand, which is what its positional Mastery Ability is for.

## Class Combat Arts

Taught by the class, kept forever, and – unlike abilities – never obtainable any other way. Because promotions are permanent, the art a class teaches is part of what the player is choosing at the fork. Mage classes appear nowhere in this section: they apply their element with every attack and do not need an art to reach the reaction system.

## Weapon Combat Arts

Tied to the weapon type, not the class: any unit that holds the weapon type can learn them. They are how a physical unit first reaches the element system, and they open at Intermediate.

**Why each weapon carries the elements it carries.** The element follows from the weapon in two layers, and the same two layers are used for every type, so a player can predict what a class can contribute to a reaction from its *Weapon Types* column alone:

- **Primary element – the physics of the strike.** What the weapon actually does to a body.
- **Secondary element – the school that wields it.** What the classes built on that weapon are for. The secondary art costs twice the primary, and is the payoff for building MP rather than spending it early.

| Weapon | Primary | Why | Secondary | Why |
| ------ | ------- | --- | --------- | --- |
| Sword | Aero | The drawn cut – an edge moving faster than the eye, a line through the air | Lux | The blade raised as a sign; the weapon of Sword Saints and Lords |
| Lance | Cryo | The thrust pins and holds; ice is both spike and stillness | Geo | The braced pike, ground held and not given |
| Axe | Geo | Mass driven downward; stone and ground split | Pyro | Friction and fury, the rending swing of the Berserker line |
| Knife | Umbra | The strike nobody saw coming | Dendro | The coated edge – the wound that keeps working |
| Gauntlet | Electro | Shock carried through the body by repeated impact | Hydro | The flowing school that redirects force instead of meeting it |
| Chain | Hydro | The chain is a wave; momentum travels its length | Electro | It arcs from one caught body to the next |
| Battle Staff | Hydro | Circular, unbroken motion; the staff never stops | Lux | The monk's staff, blessing as well as blow |
| Bow | Dendro | Wood and shaft, the hunter out of the forest, the arrow that roots | Aero | At long range the arrow rides the wind |
| Artillery | Pyro | Powder and blast; the shot burns where it lands | Geo | Shot and shrapnel – stone thrown at stone |
| Staff | Lux | The healer's light | Hydro | Water as mending |

A useful consequence: most hybrid classes hold two weapon types whose elements react with each other, so a hybrid can set up and trigger a reaction alone. Brawler and Martial Monk reach Electro and Hydro (Schockladung), Armored Knight reaches Cryo and Geo (Kristallisieren), Gladiator Geo and Hydro (Schlammfeld), Trickster Umbra and Hydro (Finsternisflut), Ranger Dendro and Umbra (Seelenfresser), Cleric Lux and Aero (Blendwirbel), Harpooner Hydro and Cryo (Gefrieren). That self-sufficiency is the hybrid's compensation for capping one rank lower in its second weapon type. The Chain is the one type whose *own* two elements react with each other (Hydro + Electro, Schockladung), so the Tamer line reaches a reaction with a single weapon type – Riptide Lash to set, Arc Chain to trigger – which is the pure line's compensation for having no second weapon at all.

## General Combat Arts

Universal techniques. Unlike abilities there are no combat-art scrolls – these are taught to every class at the tier where arts open, which is why they are deliberately plain. Their element is whichever **primary** element the wielder's weapon type carries in the table above, so a general art never gives a unit access to an element its weapon could not already apply.

| Name | Element | MP Cost | Effect |
| ---- | ------- | ------- | ------ |
| Focused Strike | Weapon primary | 4 | Double Critical rate on this attack. |
| Sure Strike | Weapon primary | 4 | The attack cannot miss and cannot critically hit. |
| Guarded Strike | Weapon primary | 5 | The attack deals half damage; until this unit's next turn it takes half damage. |
| Wide Strike | Weapon primary | 6 | Applies the element to every enemy adjacent to the target as well, without damaging them – pure setup for someone else's reaction. |
| Bonded Strike | Ally's weapon primary | 5 | Applies the primary element of an adjacent ally's weapon type instead of this unit's own. Two units standing together can produce a reaction that neither could alone. |
