# The Nexus

Dardan's Lord-Kit system – the eight abilities that run over the band between him and his allies: *Exchange*, *Lifeline*, *Heartpulse*, *Earthbound*, *Soulcairn*, *Wavelength*, *Bloodoath* and *Dawnbreak* – what each one does, what it costs, when the band is open and when it is locked, and how the system reads the affinity rank it depends on. This document holds the rules; every value lives in the [Balancing Guide → The Nexus](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus). It is the specification the game is named after.

## Overview

The Nexus is the band Dardan has felt since the orphanage – the invisible line between him and every person he trusts. In play it is **a set of eight abilities that only Dardan has, and that all act along that line**: he trades places with an ally (*Exchange*), he takes a share of an ally's wounds onto himself (*Lifeline*), he sends strength down every line at once (*Heartpulse*), – the one ability that acts on the enemy's own access to the band – he closes an enemy's access to the band (*Earthbound*), – the one ability that reaches the dead – the band keeps what the fallen had earned with him and stands beside him when no one living does (*Soulcairn*), and – the one ability that carries something back along the line – he takes an ally's element onto his blade for a few strikes (*Wavelength*), and – the one ability that runs between two allies rather than to Dardan – he binds two of them to each other for one Enemy Phase, so that each strikes where the other strikes and the battle is lost if either falls (*Bloodoath*), and – the one ability that reads how many stand with him rather than how close one of them is – once per map he strikes along a straight line whose length grows with every ranked friend near him (*Dawnbreak*). Seven of the eight read the **affinity rank** ([Affinity](Affinity.md)) between Dardan and the units they touch – Bloodoath also reads the rank the two bound allies hold with each other: the closer the bond, the further and the stronger the Nexus reaches. Dawnbreak reads the same rank the other way round: not its depth, only whether it exists, and in how many of the people around him. That is the design in one line – the mechanic the game is named after is powered by the relationships the player built.

**Exactly eight, by decision.** Dardan set the kit at **eight Nexus abilities** (2026-09-23) – the game runs on eight: eight Vigilant Knights, eight Parts of eight chapters. The eight are the ones named above. *Nexus Mastery* is a Lord-Kit **passive** and is **not** counted among them: it adds no ability of its own, it upgrades each of the eight ([Abilities → Lord Kit](../catalog/Abilities.md#lord-kit); *Core Rules → 11*). A ninth Nexus ability is not a gap in this document; it would be a change to that decision.

The purpose in one sentence: **the player decides whose burden Dardan carries this turn – whose place he takes, whose wound he shares, whose element his blade carries, which two friends he binds to each other, when he spends the one pulse, how many friends he gathers around him for the one strike – and pays for it with Dardan's own position and HP, and with Bloodoath, with the map itself.** Soulcairn adds no decision to that sentence and is not meant to: it is what the band does with a bond the player can no longer act on. Only under *Nexus Mastery* does it add one – *when* to call on the fallen, once per map (*Core Rules → 11.5*).

Under *Nexus Mastery* the *Exchange* also fires on the Enemy Phase, automatically, and the *Soulcairn* call holds across several phases (*Core Rules → 11*) |

Under *Nexus Mastery*: *Exchange* adds an automatic Enemy-Phase trigger, *Soulcairn* adds a command (*Core Rules → 11*). See *Cost* |
| Resource     | No MP, no item. A global turn cooldown on *Exchange*, *Earthbound*, *Wavelength* and *Bloodoath*; one use per chapter on *Heartpulse* and on *Dawnbreak*; Dardan's own HP on *Lifeline*; nothing on *Soulcairn* – its price was paid before it exists; on *Bloodoath*, besides the cooldown, the map – either bound unit's fall while it holds loses the battle. *Nexus Mastery* removes none of these; its Exchange trigger is once per unit per map and its Soulcairn call once per map (*Core Rules → 11*) |

Two things share the name and are **not** this system: the *Nexus* biorhythm type in [Biorhythm](Biorythm.md) is a separate per-round performance rule that happens to be Dardan's, and *Geo* in the [Magic System](Magic-System.md) is the earth element – *Earthbound* is a seal on the band, not Geo magic, and applies no element. In the same way *Dawnbreak* is named after daybreak, not after *Lux*: it is Dardan's weapon strike, applies no element and triggers no reaction (*Core Rules → 10*).

The reference Dardan named for *Earthbound* is *EarthBound* (Mother 2): its final boss does not fall to damage but to what the player built over the whole game – Paula's prayer reaching everyone the party ever met. Only that principle is taken: the Dajjal is beaten through the band, and the band is what the player has been building since Ch 01. The form of the fight is not taken; it belongs to Level 52.

## Cost

| What | Value |
| ---- | ----- |
| Resource | **None of the usual ones.** No MP – Dardan's sword line does not grow Mag, and a signature move priced in a stat its owner never builds would be a trap. No uses per map for five of the seven used abilities – a stock gets hoarded for the boss, and the Nexus is meant to be used; *Soulcairn* is passive and costs nothing at all (*Core Rules → 7*). Instead: a **global turn cooldown** on *Exchange*, *Earthbound*, *Wavelength* and *Bloodoath* (one counter each, flat, independent of rank – values in the [Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus)), **once per chapter** on *Heartpulse* (fixed by [Ch 21](../../story/chapters/Part-03-The-Champions-Road/Chapter-21-Seek-The-Light-Within.md) and the one place where uses-per-map is right: a map-wide heal without a limit would replace every healer), **once per chapter** on *Dawnbreak* too (decided by Dardan, the same limit as Heartpulse's and a rule for the same kind of reason: a multi-target strike that grows with the formation would, without a limit, become Dardan's default action every turn and replace his ordinary attack), and **Dardan's own HP** on *Lifeline* |
| Action | *Exchange*, *Lifeline* and *Wavelength* are **commands**: they end Dardan's movement for the turn but not his action – he may still attack or use an item afterwards, from the tile he now stands on. *Wavelength* is a command for exactly that reason: the element is chosen and struck with in the same turn, or the ability would be a turn spent on a promise (*Core Rules → 8*). *Heartpulse*, *Earthbound*, *Bloodoath* and *Dawnbreak* are **actions**: they are Dardan's turn. *Bloodoath* is an action for exactly that reason: the oath is the whole of Dardan's part in the turn – he does not fight beside the two he binds, and a command before it (an Exchange to place one of them, a Lifeline on one of them) is the only Nexus help he can add to it that turn (*Core Rules → 9*). *Dawnbreak* is an action because it **is** his attack for the turn: he may move first, and a command may precede it – an Exchange to put him where the line should start – but nothing follows it (*Core Rules → 10*) |
| Further cost | **Position.** After an *Exchange* Dardan stands where the ally stood – inside whatever reach the ally was in – and cannot move away. That is the price built into the form, and it is why the cooldown is flat: the swap is already paid in exposure. **Risk.** *Lifeline* has no floor – Dardan can be killed by damage dealt to someone else – and every status the ally would have received lands on Dardan instead: he can be put to sleep by an arrow aimed at someone else. **Rhythm.** A cooldown spent now is a cooldown not available two turns from now; each use is a timing decision, not a stock decision. **The fallen.** *Soulcairn* costs nothing at the moment it applies, because everything it holds was paid for before it existed: a unit, permanently, and with it every deed that unit would still have done, every rank-up it would still have taken and every bonus it would have given from a tile beside Dardan. Soulcairn returns at most what the dead had already earned, and only when nobody living is there to give more (*Core Rules → 7*). **Movement and the donor's position.** *Wavelength* ends Dardan's movement like every command, and it can only be set on an ally within that pair's rank-range – so carrying a far friend's element means standing where the band still reaches him, which is a position bought before the strike (*Core Rules → 8*). **The map.** *Bloodoath* puts the battle on two lives other than Dardan's: if either bound unit falls while the oath holds, the map is lost, and the oath **cannot be lifted early** – once sworn, the risk runs until the start of Dardan's next Player Phase, whatever the Enemy Phase brings. In Classic the map resets, so the price is the map, never the unit (*Core Rules → 9*). **The formation.** *Dawnbreak* is paid in position twice over. Its length is bought with people: every tile beyond the base is a ranked ally standing near Dardan when he strikes – a knot of units that the following Enemy Phase finds exactly where the player put it, around the unit whose fall loses the map. And its direction is bought with Dardan's own tile: the line starts where he stands, so lining it up means moving him to the end of a row of enemies, and he ends the turn there. The single use per chapter is the rest of the price – a line fired on turn three is not there for the boss's corridor (*Core Rules → 10*) |
| Visible before use | Cooldown counters and the Heartpulse use are shown on Dardan at all times. The band's colour is shown on Dardan from the first turn of the map. *Exchange* and *Lifeline* highlight the **allies** Dardan can reach (range differs per ally, so tiles are not the unit of display); an ally out of rank-range is shown with the reason. The *Lifeline* share is shown in every battle forecast that involves the linked ally – "Dardan takes X" – before the player commits to the fight. The *Earthbound* forecast lists what the target will lose. *Soulcairn*'s rank, points and element are on Dardan's affinity list at all times, and the forecast names it when it applies. *Wavelength* shows, before it is confirmed, which allies can be chosen, which element each of them lends and how many attacks that pair's rank buys; while it runs, the carried element and the attacks left are on Dardan, and every forecast shows the element the strike will apply and the reaction it would *not* trigger. *Bloodoath* shows, before it is confirmed, which pairs can be bound, each pair's Echo range, and – as part of the confirmation, not after it – the **defeat condition it adds**; while it holds, the two are marked as bound, the added defeat condition stands in the objective display, and every forecast that involves either of them says whether the Echo will fire and, if not, why. *Dawnbreak* shows its use (available / spent) on Dardan beside Heartpulse's, and before it is confirmed the **line** in each offered direction, the **count** that sets its length – which allies are counted, and which near him are not and why – the tile where a wall stops it, and every enemy it will strike with the damage each takes. Nothing about the Nexus is learned after the fact (*UI & Display*) |
| Under Nexus Mastery | Mastery takes no price away – every cooldown and every use per chapter stays (*Core Rules → 11*). What it adds carries its own limit, each shown before it matters: the Enemy-Phase *Exchange* is spent **once per ally per map**, and it puts Dardan on the ally's tile under the blow meant for the ally – recalculated against him, never fatal to him, but paid in his HP and his position for the rest of the Enemy Phase; the **second Lifeline** doubles the risk the first one carries, with no floor on either; the **Soulcairn call** is once per map, a command, and exists only because someone was lost. Each ally's rescue (available / spent) and the call's turns remaining are on screen (*UI & Display*) |

## Core Rules

### 1 – The band

**Whose it is.** The Nexus is Dardan's alone. The Nexus-Seers who could touch the band are gone – Aurevia sealed the power ([Ch 35](../../story/chapters/Part-05-Divided-We-Face-Our-Sins/Chapter-35-Where-Brilliance-Burns-Itself-Out.md)) – and the one other being that reaches it, the Dajjal, uses it **as a chain, not as a band**. So the rule that every player system must also be usable against the player is set aside here with a written reason: the enemy's version of the Nexus exists, it is the Dajjal's chain, and it is a boss mechanic of Level 52 (*Core Rules → 6*), not an ability on a class. No generic enemy exchanges, links, pulses, seals, borrows an ally's element, binds two units by an oath or strikes a line whose length its allies give it.

**Whom it reaches.** Nexus abilities target **units of the player's faction only**, in the vocabulary [Beast Summon](Beast-Summon.md#core-rules) fixed: *ally* means the player's faction, not the Other faction. **Earthbound and Dawnbreak are the two explicit exceptions** – Earthbound targets an enemy, and Dawnbreak strikes the enemies on its line; the allies Dawnbreak reads are still only the player's faction. A summoned Solmare beast is excluded from every Nexus ability: it cannot be exchanged, linked, healed by Heartpulse or sealed, and it is not counted by Dawnbreak – the beast is nobody's, and the Nexus is a bond with a person. Other-faction NPC allies are not reached either. The one case where that mattered – Elena, the hostage of Ch 08, who joins the roster only in Ch 25 – is resolved on the level's side: for Level 08 she is deployed as a player-faction unit (*Introduction*). The rule does not bend.

**Affinity rank.** *Exchange* range, *Lifeline* range, *Heartpulse* strength, *Wavelength*'s donor range and attack count and *Bloodoath*'s reach to each of the two it binds are read from the **affinity rank between Dardan and the unit** – the five-rank scale D → C → B → A → S of [Affinity](Affinity.md) – as the pair holds it **when the map begins**; points earned during the map do not raise the reach mid-battle. The per-rank tables are in the [Balancing Guide → The Nexus](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus). **Two starting ranks decided by Dardan** and recorded in the [Balancing Guide → Starting ranks](../Balancing-Guide.md#starting-ranks): the eight Vigilant Knights start at **C** with him – they grew up together – and in Ch 08 Dardan holds **B** with Elena ("sie ist ja wie eine Mutter für sie"), which is the rank the Level 08 hostage distance is built on. **Any rank from D reaches.** A unit with which Dardan holds **no** rank cannot be reached by *Exchange*, *Lifeline*, *Wavelength* or *Bloodoath* at all, and *Heartpulse* does not heal it – no rank, no band ([Affinity → Core Rules → 8](Affinity.md#8--what-nexus-reads)). **Bloodoath is the one ability that also reads a rank Dardan is not part of**: the rank the two bound allies hold with each other, which must exist and which sets their Echo range – read, like every rank here, as it stood when the map began (*Core Rules → 9*). **Dawnbreak is the one ability that reads the rank without its depth**: it asks only whether a unit near Dardan holds a rank with him at all – D counts as much as S – and counts how many do (*Core Rules → 10*); the rank it asks about is, like every rank here, the one the pair held when the map began. The Nexus feeds the bond back: Exchange, Lifeline and Heartpulse are affinity sources for Dardan and the unit they touch; Earthbound, Soulcairn, Wavelength, Bloodoath and Dawnbreak are not.

**State: golden or black.** On every map the band is in one of two states, shown on Dardan from turn one:

| Band | Meaning | Set by |
| ---- | ------- | ------ |
| **Golden** | The Nexus is available – every ability Dardan holds can be used | Default |
| **Black** | The Nexus is **locked for this chapter** – no Nexus ability can be used, cooldowns do not matter, and *Heartpulse*'s chapter use is not spent | The chapter's **level box**, written by Lorekeeper. The rule is that a chapter may lock the band; which chapters do is the story's decision and is not listed here |

A third condition is not a state: before Ch 08 the player does not hold the Nexus yet, and nothing is shown – Ch 01 and Ch 05 perform the swap in prose, and Level 05 in particular must **not** offer it (*Introduction*). The story currently shows the band grey at the end of Ch 40A, black from Ch 41 and golden again in Ch 44 after the trial, and Ch 30's box already switches the Exchange off. **The list of black chapters is not a gap in this specification – it is a pending story revision:** the chapters were written before the band had a state, and Dardan will revise them; the level boxes are the place where the answer will appear (*Open decisions → 1*). Dardan is absent from Part 06 (Hasan's strand), so the Nexus does not exist in Levels 33B–40B at all.

**Nexus Mastery** (Lord Kit, [Abilities](../catalog/Abilities.md#lord-kit)) is the kit's late payoff, unlocked **in Part 08** (Ch 49–52) – the exact trigger is open (*Open decisions → 4*). It **upgrades each of the eight Nexus abilities individually**: every ability gets exactly its own upgrade, written out in *Core Rules → 11*, and nothing more. Decided by Dardan, 2026-09-24. This **replaces** the two blanket rules Mastery carried before: **no cooldown is removed** – Exchange, Earthbound, Wavelength and Bloodoath keep theirs, and Heartpulse and Dawnbreak keep their one use per chapter – and **no range grows by default**. The one Mastery lever, the *Nexus Mastery range bonus* in the [Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus-mastery), is added exactly where an ability's own upgrade names it: the Exchange range, the Lifeline range, Wavelength's donor range, both of Bloodoath's ranges, and Dawnbreak's counting radius. The Tower is where the band reaches furthest and where it answers on the Enemy Phase too – which is what the Dajjal fight is built on (*Core Rules → 6*).

### 2 – Exchange

Dardan and one allied unit **trade places**.

1. On his turn – before or after moving, but before his action – Dardan chooses *Exchange* while its cooldown is at 0.
2. He chooses an allied unit whose distance from him is within the **Exchange range for the affinity rank he holds with that unit** ([Balancing Guide → Exchange range by affinity rank](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus)). Distance is counted in tiles and **nothing in between matters** – enemies, walls, doors, magical barriers. The band goes through everything; in [Ch 05](../../story/chapters/Part-01-Path-Of-Liberation/Chapter-05-A-Heart-Of-Gold.md) it goes through the Guardian's barrier.
3. **Both landing tiles must be legal for the unit that lands on them** – Move Type against terrain, as the Movement rules (backlog) define it. An Infantry Dardan cannot take a Flying ally's tile over water; a Cavalry ally cannot take Dardan's tile if a horse may not stand there. If either tile is illegal, the swap is not offered.
4. **The ally's condition is irrelevant.** A sleeping, paralysed, netted or otherwise status-bound ally can be swapped. An ally that has already acted this turn can be swapped.
5. The two trade places. **Dardan's movement for the turn is over** – he may still attack or use an item from the new tile, he may not move, and no post-action movement (Canto, Wind Walk or any effect worded as "move again") applies to him this turn. **The ally keeps its own action** if it has not used it yet, and may move and act normally from Dardan's old tile.
6. The *Exchange* cooldown starts. It is **one global counter, flat** – the same number of turns whoever was swapped, whatever the rank. It counts down by one at the start of each of Dardan's Player Phases. Rank scales range; nothing scales the cooldown. That is the single-lever design: the bond decides how far the band reaches, never how often.

*Exchange* is a Player-Phase command. Without *Nexus Mastery*, Dardan never swaps on the Enemy Phase; the involuntary swap of Ch 01 is prose, not a rule. Under Mastery the command itself is unchanged – it keeps its cooldown and stays once per turn (conservative; *Open decisions → 3*) – and Exchange gains its range bonus and one Enemy-Phase trigger (*Core Rules → 11.1*).

### 3 – Lifeline

Dardan ties the band to one ally and **carries a share of every wound that ally takes – and every affliction, whole**.

1. On his turn, before his action, Dardan chooses *Lifeline* and an allied unit within the **Lifeline range for the affinity rank he holds with that unit** ([Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus)); range is counted as for *Exchange*, through everything. Placing it is a command: Dardan's movement ends, his action remains.
2. **Damage is shared.** Whenever the linked ally **takes damage from an attack** – either phase, any attacker – the damage is split at the moment it is dealt: the **Lifeline share** ([Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus)) is taken from Dardan's HP, the remainder from the ally's. The damage is computed once, against the ally's Def or Res and terrain, with every reduction the ally has (Pavise, Bulwark, a shield); the split is applied to the result. Dardan's own Def and Res are **not** applied to his share – the wound already happened, he is only carrying it. Dardan's share is rounded down and the remainder stays on the ally (conservative; *Open decisions → 5*).
3. **Status effects are not shared – they go to Dardan instead.** Whenever the linked ally **would receive a status effect** – poison, sleep, a debuff, anything the Status Effects system (backlog) calls a status – the ally does not receive it; **Dardan does, alone and in full.** Not both, not a share: the affliction lands on him as if he had been the target. Whatever the status does from then on – a poison tick each round, a turn lost to sleep, −4 Def for three rounds – it does to Dardan, and the ally is untouched. A status Dardan is immune to (Sanctuary, Hexward, Aurelian Ward, any immunity) **fizzles** – it reaches neither of them (conservative; *Open decisions → 9*). An **applied element** in the Magic System's sense is not a status and stays on the ally (conservative; *Open decisions → 10*).
4. **There is no floor.** If Dardan's share is his remaining HP or more, Dardan falls, with everything permadeath means. That is the point: a burden that cannot kill him is not a decision. Conversely, a blow that would have killed the ally may not, because part of it is Dardan's now. Poison redirected to him keeps the [poison floor](../Balancing-Guide.md#5--poison) like any poison – it softens him, it does not kill him; the attack that follows might.
5. **It lasts until Dardan lifts it.** Lifting is a free action on Dardan's turn, any time, no range. It also ends when Dardan falls, when the ally falls, or when the map ends – Lifeline never carries over to the next chapter. A status Dardan took on stays on him after the line is lifted; the band moved it, it does not move it back.
6. **One Lifeline at a time.** Placing it on a second ally is not offered while the first stands; lift first (conservative; *Open decisions → 6*). Under *Nexus Mastery*, two at once (*Core Rules → 11.2*).
7. **Distance after placement is irrelevant.** Once placed, the line holds wherever the two units go – the rank-range is checked only when it is placed (conservative; *Open decisions → 7*).

Damage that is neither an attack nor a status – a Burning Field the ally stands on, Juggernaut's passage, any effect worded as "takes damage" outside a combat and outside a status – stays with the ally (conservative; *Open decisions → 8*). Lifeline has no cooldown and no use limit: its whole price is Dardan's HP, his condition, and the command (*Open decisions → 11*).

### 4 – Heartpulse

A wave from Dardan that **heals every allied unit on the map** at once.

1. On his turn, Dardan chooses *Heartpulse* as his action, once per chapter. No target is chosen.
2. **Every unit of the player's faction on the map that holds an affinity rank with Dardan** recovers HP – regardless of distance, line of sight, walls or the state the unit is in. Range does not scale; the pulse reaches "jeden den das Band kannte" – and only those.
3. **How much each unit recovers is read from that unit's affinity rank with Dardan** – per unit, not from an average ([Balancing Guide → Heartpulse strength by affinity rank](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus)); a unit with no rank recovers nothing. A unit Dardan is close to is healed more than one he barely knows; in the labyrinth of [Ch 21](../../story/chapters/Part-03-The-Champions-Road/Chapter-21-Seek-The-Light-Within.md) the lines run "von Dardan aus in alle Richtungen … jeder führte zu einem Paar", each its own. Per-unit is the conservative reading of the scene (*Open decisions → 12*).
4. Dardan himself is included, at the value the table sets for him (conservative; *Open decisions → 13*). Overheal is lost. Heartpulse cures nothing and grants nothing but HP – until *Nexus Mastery*, under which it also cures and heals one row higher (*Core Rules → 11.3*).
5. *Heartpulse* is Dardan's action for the turn. It does not touch the *Exchange* or *Earthbound* cooldown. *Nexus Mastery* leaves its once per chapter standing.

The single use per chapter is a **rule, not a tuning value**: it is what the chapter's level box fixed, and it is the boundary that keeps healers in the game. A second use would be a different system.

### 5 – Earthbound

Dardan **closes one enemy's access to the band**.

1. On his turn, Dardan chooses *Earthbound* as his action while its cooldown is at 0, and one enemy unit within its **fixed range** ([Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus) – proposed 1–2). No rank exists with an enemy, so nothing scales.
2. **No hit roll.** The seal is set; it is a cooldown ability, and a cooldown that can whiff is a turn lost to dice, which [Pillar 5](../Design-Pillars.md) forbids.
3. For the **seal duration** ([Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus)) the sealed unit loses **everything that runs through the Nexus**:
   - every **ability** it holds – class, mastery, personal, scroll; passive ones stop working for the duration, including flat stat passives and Canto (conservative; *Open decisions → 14*);
   - every **combat art**;
   - every **tome and staff** – it cannot cast, it cannot heal, it cannot use a staff of any kind;
   - every **aura** it projects – Sanctuary, Bulwark, Wall of Spears, a Light or Shadow Field it carries – stops at its tile. Auras that *other* enemies project still cover it.
   
   What remains is the **plain weapon strike**: it moves, it attacks and counter-attacks with a physical weapon at that weapon's range, the weapon triangle applies, it may use items. A sealed bishop cannot heal, a sealed Bestiarius cannot call and cannot order the beasts it already called, a sealed boss loses its mastery – but it still stands, and it still hits. A unit whose only weapons are tomes or staves cannot attack at all while sealed.
4. The seal lasts **N rounds**: set on round R, it expires at the start of the Player Phase of round R + N. The turns remaining are shown on the unit.
5. The *Earthbound* cooldown starts – one global counter, flat, counted down like the Exchange's. Nothing an enemy does can lift a seal early; nothing on the enemy side touches the band.

*Earthbound* **closes; it never controls.** The Guru's line in [Ch 44](../../story/chapters/Part-07-Before-The-Storm/Chapter-44-Must-Face-The-One-Who-Refuses-To-Fall.md) – "dem Band vertrauen statt es zu kontrollieren" – is the boundary of the design: the seal is not a hold (Tripwire, Ensnare, Grapple and Maike's binding already hold), not a charm, not a command over the enemy's action. It takes away what the enemy reaches through the band and leaves the enemy itself. In-world it is what Aurevia did to the Nexus-Seers – "Sie hat die Kraft aus dem Nexus genommen und eingeschlossen" ([Ch 35](../../story/chapters/Part-05-Divided-We-Face-Our-Sins/Chapter-35-Where-Brilliance-Burns-Itself-Out.md)) – done to one unit, for a short time. It is not Geo magic: no element is applied, no reaction is triggered, Res is not consulted. Under *Nexus Mastery* the seal also closes on every enemy adjacent to the target (*Core Rules → 11.4*); the boundary above holds unchanged.

### 6 – Earthbound and the Dajjal

The Dajjal holds his units on the band **as a chain**: "die Verbindung zwischen allem Leben als Kette statt als Band" ([Ch 35](../../story/chapters/Part-05-Divided-We-Face-Our-Sins/Chapter-35-Where-Brilliance-Burns-Itself-Out.md)); his will "floss durch sie alle wie ein einziger Gedanke" ([Ch 48](../../story/chapters/Part-07-Before-The-Storm/Chapter-48-End-Of-The-World.md)). Dardan's decision for the fight, written here so that Level 52 is designed against it:

- **Every unit the Dajjal holds on the chain transfers its life to his.** Damage dealt to the Dajjal is healed immediately from the chained units, and **the chained reinforcements never stop.** Killing the chained units is a treadmill – the chain is *Lifeline* turned the wrong way round, a bond that feeds the master instead of carrying the follower.
- **Earthbound cuts the chain for its duration.** A sealed Dajjal has no access to the band: nothing flows to him from his units, and the window in which he is vulnerable is exactly the seal's duration. The fight is won inside that window, by the tool the player has been building since Ch 08.

How many units hang on the chain, how much of each wound is healed, how often the reinforcements come, whether the fight ends by damage inside the window or by a final seal, and whether the Dajjal can be sealed at all before some condition is met – **all of that is Level 52** and belongs to `levelcraft`, not here. This document fixes only the two sentences above.

### 7 – Soulcairn

The band **keeps what the fallen had earned with Dardan**, and stands beside him when no one living does. Decided by Dardan, 2026-09-21.

1. **Passive, Dardan only, Classic only.** Soulcairn is held like the rest of the kit – no Capacity, cannot be unequipped (*Open decisions → 15*) – and never used: it has no command, no action, no target, no range, no cooldown. The one exception is *Nexus Mastery*, which gives it a once-per-map command (*Core Rules → 11.5*); everything in this section holds outside that call. It exists only under permadeath. In Casual nothing is ever lost, so its pool is always empty and the ability does nothing; it is not shown there.
2. **The pool.** Every playable unit that has **fallen** – lost permanently, in Classic – leaves its pair with Dardan on the chart, frozen at the points it held at the moment of its death ([Affinity → Interaction → Permadeath / Casual](Affinity.md#interaction-with-other-mechanics), decided with this rule). Soulcairn's pool is the **sum of those frozen points** over every fallen unit. A pair that had not reached D still had points, and those count too – the pool sums points, not ranks. The pool only ever grows, and only by a death; nothing the player does adds to it. Only playable units of the roster count – no Other-faction NPC, no summoned beast, no story character has a pair with Dardan to leave behind ([Affinity → Prerequisites](Affinity.md#prerequisites)).
3. **One partner, one rank.** On Dardan's affinity list the fallen together are **one partner, named *Soulcairn***. Its rank is read from the pool against the ordinary [rank thresholds](../Balancing-Guide.md#rank-thresholds) – the same rows as every living pair, capped at S. It is not throttled and not chosen: no rank-up is spent on it, because nobody chooses it and it competes with no one for Dardan's one rise per chapter – the fallen earned it already. Two consequences to read off the thresholds: **three D-rank deaths do not make a C** (three pairs at the D threshold sum to less than the C threshold), and **a single A-rank death is alone an A**. Losing more people is never a shortcut through the ranks; losing one person the player was close to is exactly as heavy as that bond was.
4. **The bonus – only when nobody living is there.** Soulcairn grants the ordinary [affinity combat bonus](Affinity.md#4--the-combat-bonus) for its rank, entered into the formulas as the *Affinity Bonus* term like any partner's – **but only in a combat in which no living affinity partner of Dardan's with at least rank D stands within the bonus range**. This is a gate, not a tie-break: a living partner at D beats Soulcairn at S. Whenever a living ranked partner is in range, that partner's bonus applies and Soulcairn is silent. The rule "only the strongest partner in range counts" ([Affinity → 4](Affinity.md#4--the-combat-bonus)) is unchanged – Soulcairn is simply the partner that is always in range and always loses to any living one. **It fills the gap, it never stacks.** Only Dardan receives it; a partner with one end has one end.
5. **The element.** A bonus's kind is the sum of two element mixes ([Affinity → 4](Affinity.md#4--the-combat-bonus)); Soulcairn's second element is the element of the **heaviest fallen unit** – the one whose frozen pair with Dardan holds the most points. Every later death re-reads this: if the new fallen unit's pair outweighs the old one's, the element changes. **That is intended** – the cairn is built of whoever weighs most on it. Tie: equal points → **the later death**. Dardan's own element is his sheet's, as in every pair.
6. **When it moves.** The pool, the rank and the element are re-read at the moment a unit falls, and the row on Dardan's list moves with it. Whether the new rank applies in the map on which the death happened or – like every other pair's rank – from the next map on is written in its conservative form under *Open decisions → 21*. A unit lost on Hasan's strand in Ch 33B–40B enters the pool when the chart's two halves rejoin in Ch 41, as its greyed link does ([Affinity → UI](Affinity.md#ui--display)); on Hasan's strand no Nexus exists, so the question does not arise there.
7. **What it is not.** Soulcairn is not a unit: it has no tile, cannot be exchanged, linked, healed or sealed, earns no points, holds no Heart-to-Hearts, takes no map talk and never appears on the rank-up screen. It reads no rank of its own into Exchange range, Lifeline range or Heartpulse – it is a combat bonus and nothing else. It never contributes to a Chain Attack, a shared kill or any other source; the dead do no deeds. With no losses the partner does not exist – pool zero, no row, no node. That is not a gap in the system: it is the design, and the best outcome.

**Why it does not make losses desirable – the argument the design rests on.** Three things, each sufficient on its own. *A living partner at the same rank always beats it:* the living partner gives the same bonus from a tile beside Dardan *and* keeps earning, rising, talking and fighting; Soulcairn gives the same bonus only where the living one would have, and nothing else. A player who trades a living A for a Soulcairn A has traded a unit for the absence of one. *The pool is at most what the dead had already earned:* nothing is added at the death, nothing is multiplied, and the thresholds make sure that many shallow losses do not sum to one deep bond. The player cannot build Soulcairn – he can only stop building everything else. *It takes nothing from anyone:* no living pair loses points, no rank-up is spent, no Nexus reach changes. It is the affinity bonus the player would otherwise have lost entirely, returned in its weakest form – present only in the one situation the affinity system already punishes: Dardan fighting alone. The player who reads the rules sees that the strongest Soulcairn possible is a roster of dead friends, and that this roster loses every fight the living one would have won.

**Rejected on the way, recorded so the question is not reopened.** *Circle* – every living partner within range stacks its bonus: rejected as too strong, and it would overturn the no-stacking rule that keeps clumping unpaid. *Watch* – a guaranteed block per fallen unit: rejected as too strong; a stock of free survivals that grows with each death is the one shape that would make losses desirable. Both were Dardan's to reject, and he did (2026-09-21).

### 8 – Wavelength

Dardan takes **an ally's element onto his blade** for a few strikes. Decided by Dardan, 2026-09-22.

1. **A command with a cooldown, Dardan only.** Held like the rest of the kit – no Capacity, never unequipped (*Open decisions → 15*) – and available in every mode; nothing about it is Classic-gated. On his turn, before his action and while its cooldown is at 0, Dardan chooses *Wavelength*. It is a **command**: his movement for the turn ends, his action remains, and he may strike with the borrowed element in the same turn. The cooldown is a **global flat counter** like the Exchange's; *how long* it runs is a tuning value in the [Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus), *that it is a cooldown and not a use per chapter* is the rule – an element the player hoards for the boss is an element he never learns to set.
2. **The donor.** One **living** unit of the player's faction with which Dardan holds an affinity rank – any rank from D – within that rank's **range along the band**, counted as for *Exchange* and *Lifeline*: in tiles, through enemies, walls and barriers, because it is the same band ([Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus); whether the table is shared or its own row is *Open decisions → 28*). A unit with no rank lends nothing; a summoned beast is never a donor (*Interaction*); the *Soulcairn* partner is not a donor either, in the conservative reading (*Open decisions → 25*).
3. **What is borrowed: the element, and nothing else.** Dardan's ordinary weapon attacks carry the donor's **elemental affinity** – the element the donor's sheet gives it, which [Affinity → Core Rules → 7](Affinity.md#7--elemental-affinity-and-who-has-none) calls *what the unit brings to a bond* – and **apply** it to the target in the Magic System's sense, an applied element like any other. What they do not do is light it (*→ 7*). **No ability, no combat art, no stat, no growth and no weapon rank is borrowed.** That boundary is the reason the rejection of **Skill Links** still stands ([Affinity → What Affinity is not](Affinity.md#what-affinity-is-not-skill-links-rejected)): a borrowed *ability* would blur the mechanical translation of who a character is – Leona with Hasan's ability is less Leona – while a borrowed *element* is the one thing the bond system already says is carried between two people. Dardan with Hasan's Pyro is still Dardan; he is holding what Hasan brings.
4. **How many attacks, and how the rank enters.** The borrowed element lasts for a **number of attacks**, and the only rule is that this number **rises monotonically with the affinity rank** – a bond the player built deeper carries the element further. The D–S row is a tuning value and lives in the [Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus) beside Exchange's range and Heartpulse's healing; no number belongs here. The count runs across phases: Dardan's counter-attacks on the Enemy Phase carry the element and spend from the same count, and it ends when the count is spent, when Dardan falls or when the map ends – Wavelength never carries into the next chapter. Which attacks spend from the count is *Open decisions → 30*, in its conservative form: **an attack that lands spends one; an attack that misses applies nothing and spends nothing.**
5. **The element is chosen once and does not change mid-combat.** It is fixed at the moment of the command. Dardan cannot swap donors while the count runs; a second *Wavelength* needs the cooldown, and it replaces the carried element outright rather than adding a second one. He never carries two elements at once – that is the Arcanist's job (*Convergence*, [Abilities](../catalog/Abilities.md)), and it stays there. That holds under *Nexus Mastery* too: a borrowed element replaces the fallen element of a running *Soulcairn* call until its count is spent (*Core Rules → 11.5*), and Mastery widens where the element lands, not how many he carries (*Core Rules → 11.6*).
6. **The one written exception in the Magic System – and why it is allowed exactly once.** [Magic System → Wie Elemente appliziert werden](Magic-System.md#wie-elemente-appliziert-werden) says: a mage applies with every attack, a non-mage only through a **Combat Art**. Wavelength is the single exception to that rule, and the exception is named there as well as here. It is allowed because it is bounded on every side that matters: it belongs to **one unit in the game**, it costs a **command and a cooldown**, it runs for a **counted** number of attacks and not for a map, it needs a **living ally in rank-range** who lends the element, and – decisively – it applies but **never triggers** (*→ 7*). Dardan does not become a mage; he becomes a **setter**. Anything broader – a second unit, a permanent state, or applying *and* igniting – would not be an exception but a second magic system.
7. **No reactions: he sets, he never triggers.** Dardan stays a non-mage. A Wavelength attack onto an enemy that already carries an applied element does its damage and **the element stays on the enemy** – exactly the *Normaler Angriff (Nicht-Magier) auf Element* row of the Magic System's table. The reaction is somebody else's to light: the mage behind him, an ally's Combat Art, or Dardan's own Combat Art under the art's own rules, which this ability does not change (*Open decisions → 29* for the one edge it leaves: an art that carries no element of its own).
8. **What it is not.** Not a buff: it changes what his attacks *apply*, not what they *do* – no damage, hit, crit or defence is altered by the borrowing, and the element's own damage rules are the Magic System's. Not a bond source: Wavelength earns the pair no affinity points (*Interaction*), because the donor performs no deed – the band takes, and this time Dardan is the one who is carried. Not a reach ability for anyone else: the donor is untouched, keeps its element, its turn and its own attacks, and does not even have to be able to act.

**Why the decision the player makes is a real one.** The cost is a command, a cooldown and Dardan's movement; the gain is a single element on a few strikes. That makes *which* element the whole question, and the answer is never the same twice: Pyro onto the enemy the Hydro Sage will hit next turn, Cryo onto the one the front line needs slowed, Geo where a Dendro ally is waiting. Because the count rises with rank, the friends Dardan built the deepest bonds with are the ones whose element he can carry through a whole Enemy Phase – and because he can never trigger, every use of Wavelength is a sentence that only somebody else can finish.

### 9 – Bloodoath

Dardan **binds two allies to each other** until his next Player Phase: each strikes where the other strikes, the first kill lets its killer act again, and if either of them falls, the battle is lost. Decided by Dardan, 2026-09-23. The reference he named is *Fire Emblem Awakening*'s **Dual Strike** – a partner joining the attack – and, for the act-again reward, **Galeforce**. What is taken from Dual Strike is the shape (the partner strikes the same target); what is not taken is its roll: the Echo is guaranteed whenever its conditions hold, because a reward that procs is information the player cannot plan with ([Pillar 5](../Design-Pillars.md)). And the distance is not adjacency but the pair's own rank.

1. **An action with a cooldown, Dardan only.** Held like the rest of the kit – no Capacity, never unequipped (*Open decisions → 15*) – and available in **Classic and Casual** alike. On his turn, while its cooldown is at 0 and the band is golden, Dardan chooses *Bloodoath* as his **action**: he may move first, and a command may precede it (*Prerequisites → Turn state*), but nothing follows it – the turn is spent on the oath. The cooldown is a **global flat counter** like the Exchange's, counted down the same way at the start of each of Dardan's Player Phases; *how long* it runs is a tuning value in the [Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#bloodoath), *that it is a cooldown and not a use per chapter* is the rule. A black band locks it with the rest of the kit.
2. **The two.** Dardan chooses **two different living units of the player's faction**. Neither may be Dardan himself, a summoned beast, an Other-faction unit or the *Soulcairn* partner. For **each** of the two, Dardan must hold an affinity rank with it – any rank from D – and it must stand within **the range that rank gives**, counted in tiles through enemies, walls and barriers, as for *Exchange* and *Lifeline* ([Balancing Guide → Bloodoath](../Balancing-Guide.md#bloodoath); whether that is the shared band table or a row of its own is *Open decisions → 32*). And **the two must hold an affinity rank with each other** – any rank from D. All three ranks are read as they stood when the map began (*Core Rules → 1*). The condition of the two is irrelevant, as for *Exchange*: a sleeping, held or already-acted unit can be bound – it simply delivers no Echo while it cannot attack (*→ 4*). Once sworn, the distance to Dardan no longer matters; the oath holds wherever the three go, as a placed Lifeline does.
3. **Duration: exactly one Enemy Phase.** The oath holds from the moment it is sworn until **the start of Dardan's next Player Phase** – the rest of this Player Phase and the one Enemy Phase that follows. **It cannot be lifted early**: not by Dardan, not by either bound unit, not by any ability. It ends before that moment only when the map ends, and it never carries into the next chapter. Because it is an action on a cooldown and lasts less than a round, there is never more than one oath on the map.
4. **The Echo – the reward.** Whenever one bound unit **attacks** an enemy – on the Player Phase, or with a counter-attack on the Enemy Phase – **the other bound unit (its partner) strikes the same target once**, provided that, at that moment:
   - the partner stands within **the Echo range of the pair's own rank with each other** – the distance between the two bound units, in tiles, through everything ([Balancing Guide → Bloodoath](../Balancing-Guide.md#bloodoath)). **The distance is checked at every Echo, not when the oath is sworn.** A partner out of range delivers nothing this time; the oath, and its risk, remain;
   - the target still stands after the attacker's combat;
   - the partner could make an ordinary attack right now with its **own equipped weapon** – it has one, a mage has the MP an attack costs, and no status stops it from attacking. A staff-only unit, or a unit with nothing equipped, cannot echo. The target does **not** have to be within that weapon's range: the band carries the strike, as the reference's partner joins from beside the attacker, not from beside the target.

   Nothing is rolled for the Echo to fire: if the three conditions hold, it fires. An attack counts whether it hits or misses; a staff use is not an attack. **One Echo per combat**, resolved when the bound unit's combat is over (conservative; *Open decisions → 33*).
   
   **The echo strike** is **one ordinary strike of the partner's own equipped weapon** (conservative; *Open decisions → 34*): the weapon's own numbers, the triangle, the ordinary hit and critical roll, and the cost an ordinary attack of that weapon has. Never a combat art. Three things are decided and hold whatever *Open decisions → 34* settles: the echo strike **cannot be countered**; it **does not wear down a boss's shield** – breaking the shield stays the [Chain Attack](Chain-Attack.md)'s work, and a guaranteed extra strike on every attack would otherwise be the fastest shield-breaker in the game; and it **does not itself trigger an Echo** – the partner's echo strike is not an attack by a bound unit for this rule, or the two would echo each other without end.
5. **Act again – the Galeforce reward.** On the **Player Phase**, the **first enemy killed by either bound unit** – the killing blow dealt by one of them, in its own attack or in its echo strike – **refreshes the unit that landed the killing blow**: it may **move and act again** this Player Phase, as if it had not yet moved (the Galeforce reading; *Open decisions → 35*). If the killing blow was the partner's echo strike, **the partner is refreshed**, even if it had already acted. This happens **once per Bloodoath** and is then spent for both, whether the refreshed unit uses its second turn or not. Kills on the Enemy Phase neither refresh nor spend it. A refreshed unit's second action is still under the oath – it echoes and is echoed as before – but it cannot earn a second refresh. Dardan is never refreshed: he swears, he is never bound. Kills inside a Chain Attack are *Open decisions → 38*. Under *Nexus Mastery* the refresh is once per bound unit instead of once per oath (*Core Rules → 11.7*).
6. **The risk – a defeat condition.** If either bound unit **falls while the oath holds, the map is lost**, exactly as it is lost when Dardan falls. The consequences, decided with the rule:
   - **In Classic the map resets.** The bound unit is therefore never permanently lost to the oath – its fall ends the battle before the battle can take it – and **a bound unit's death never enters Soulcairn's pool** (*Core Rules → 7*). The stake is the map, not the unit.
   - **In Casual** the map is lost the same way; the oath does not change what Casual is.
   - Every ordinary way of not falling works: Miracle, a shield, a Lifeline share (*Interaction*). Only the fall itself triggers the defeat.
   - **This is the first mechanic that adds a defeat condition during a battle.** Level documents set their defeat conditions before the first turn; Bloodoath creates one by a player action mid-map and removes it at a set moment. **Victory & Defeat Conditions** is still on the [mechanics backlog](README.md#backlog--not-yet-documented), and it inherits the requirement from here: a defeat condition that a player action can add, that ends at a stated moment, and that is shown from the moment it exists. This document does not specify that system (*Open decisions → 40*).
7. **The enemy does not know.** The enemy AI does **not** treat bound units specially – it neither hunts them nor avoids them; it targets as it would without the oath. And there is **no defensive half**: no Dual Guard, no shared block, no damage split – the oath strikes, it does not shield. The reason the AI stays blind: the risk is meant to be the player's own positioning, which the danger overlay already shows truthfully; an AI that focused bound units would turn a readable risk into a guess about machine behaviour. No enemy swears an oath (*Core Rules → 1*).
8. **What it is not.** Not a buff – no stat, hit, avoid or bonus is changed on either unit; the whole reward is the extra strike and the one refresh. Not a Chain Attack – it breaks no shield and needs no formation of three. Not a bond source – swearing earns nothing, and the strikes and kills made under it count only as [Affinity](Affinity.md#2--earning-points-deeds-never-proximity) already counts any attack (*Interaction*; *Open decisions → 36*). And not Dardan's fight: he is the one who binds, never one of the bound.

**Why the decision the player makes is a real one.** The oath asks two questions at once, and they pull against each other. *Where will the enemy come?* – because the Echo pays most on the Enemy Phase, on every counter the bound front-liner throws, so the pair wants to stand where the enemy is. *Can both of them survive it?* – because if either falls, everything is lost, and the oath cannot be taken back once the Enemy Phase shows what it brings. The player who binds the two units already standing safe gets little; the player who binds the wall and the archer behind it gets a counter and a second arrow on every enemy that touches the wall – and has staked the map on the wall. On the Player Phase the order becomes the puzzle: swear first, then attack, and aim the first kill at the unit whose second turn is worth the most. And the bond enters twice: Dardan's rank decides whom he can bind, the pair's own rank decides how far apart they may stand – so it is the bonds his friends built with each other, not with him, that decide how the oath can be used.

### 10 – Dawnbreak

Dardan **strikes along a straight line**, once per map, and the line is as long as the number of friends who stand with him. Decided by Dardan, 2026-09-23. The reference he named is *Xenoblade Chronicles*' **Monado Buster**; what is taken is exactly one thing – a signature line attack whose power comes from the party standing with the hero. The [Affinity](Affinity.md) system already follows *Xenoblade Chronicles 1*, so the reference sits on ground the game already stands on. Nothing else of the Monado is taken. The name is *Dawnbreak*, not *Dawnbreaker*: every Nexus ability is named after what happens, not after the one who does it.

1. **An action, once per chapter, Dardan only.** Held like the rest of the kit – no Capacity, never unequipped (*Open decisions → 15*) – and available in **Classic and Casual** alike. On his turn, while it has not yet been used this chapter and the band is golden, Dardan chooses *Dawnbreak* as his **action**: he may move first, and a command may precede it (*Prerequisites → Turn state*), but nothing follows it – the strike is his attack for the turn. **Once per chapter is a rule, not a tuning value**, the same limit as Heartpulse's: a second use would be a different system. A black band locks it with the rest of the kit, and, as with Heartpulse, the chapter's use is not spent on a locked map. Dawnbreak and Heartpulse are counted separately – using one never spends the other – but both are actions, so they are never used in the same turn.
2. **The count – who stands with him.** At the moment Dardan chooses Dawnbreak, every unit is counted that
   - is a **living** unit of the **player's faction**, deployed on this map;
   - holds an **affinity rank with Dardan** – any rank from D, as the pair held it when the map began (*Core Rules → 1*);
   - stands within the Affinity **[bonus range](../Balancing-Guide.md#bonus-range)** of Dardan – the distance Dardan already set for the affinity combat bonus, counted in tiles as [Affinity → 4](Affinity.md#4--the-combat-bonus) counts it, with nothing in between mattering. The value lives there and nowhere else; tuning the bonus range tunes this count with it. Under *Nexus Mastery* the radius is the bonus range plus the Nexus Mastery range bonus (*Core Rules → 11.8*).

   The unit's condition is irrelevant, as for *Exchange*: a sleeping, held or already-acted ally counts. **The rank does not matter: D counts exactly as S, and every counted unit is one.** Never counted: Dardan himself, a summoned beast, any Other-faction unit, and the *Soulcairn* partner. Dardan's own framing for the last, as flavour and not as rule: *one could argue that Soulcairn is already in the line – it is the base.* The base is what Dardan carries when nobody stands with him.
3. **The length.** The line is **a base length, plus one equal step for every counted unit, up to a cap** – base, step and cap were set by Dardan as rules and are recorded in the [Balancing Guide → Dawnbreak](../Balancing-Guide.md#dawnbreak), not here. Every counted unit adds the same step; that is "everyone counts as one" in its mechanical form. The count – and with it the length – is taken when the ability is chosen and shown before it is confirmed (conservative; *Open decisions → 43*); nothing that happens during the strike changes it.
4. **The line.** It starts on the tile **next to Dardan** in the chosen direction and runs straight, tile by tile, for its length. The directions are the **four grid directions** (conservative; *Open decisions → 42*). It ends early at the **map edge**, and at the first **wall**: a tile no unit of any Move Type may enter – walls, closed doors, pillars and whatever the Movement rules (backlog) make impassable to fliers as well – or a magical barrier. The line stops **before** that tile and strikes nothing on or beyond it. Terrain that only some Move Types cannot cross – water, chasms, cliffs – does not stop it, and an enemy flier standing over it is struck; elevation is not a wall (conservative; *Open decisions → 49*). **This is the one place in the kit where walls matter**, and deliberately: every other Nexus ability goes through everything because the band carries it; Dawnbreak is Dardan's own strike, it travels through the world, and the world can stop it. The band gives it its length, not its path.
5. **Who is struck.** Every **enemy** unit on a tile of the line is struck, once each; the line does not stop at the first one. **Allies are not harmed**: the line passes through units of the player's faction untouched, and – conservatively – through Other-faction units and summoned beasts as well, which are not enemies of Dardan (*Open decisions → 49*). A unit hidden from the player is passed through as well, so that what the preview shows is exactly what is struck (*→ 49*). A direction whose line holds no enemy is not offered, and if no direction holds one, Dawnbreak is not offered at all (conservative; *Open decisions → 43*): a once-per-chapter action is not spent on empty tiles by a misclick.
6. **The strike.** Against each enemy struck, Dardan deals the damage of **one ordinary strike of his equipped weapon** against that target (conservative; *Open decisions → 41*): the weapon's own numbers and effectiveness, the triangle, the target's terrain, Def or Res as the weapon calls for – the [damage formula](../Balancing-Guide.md#-damage-calculation-formula), unchanged, computed per target. One strike per target, never a follow-up for Attack Speed, never a combat art. **How strong the strike is relative to that ordinary strike is a lever in the [Balancing Guide](../Balancing-Guide.md#dawnbreak) and is not yet set**; until it is, the ordinary strike is the rule. Dardan must have a weapon equipped with which he could make an ordinary attack, or Dawnbreak is not offered. Three things hold whatever the lever says:
   - **No roll.** Every enemy on the line is struck, for the damage the preview shows – no hit roll and no critical roll (conservative; *Open decisions → 44*). The kit's standing rule is that no Nexus ability misses on a roll ([Pillar 5](../Design-Pillars.md)), and a once-per-chapter action that could whiff against half its line would be the chapter's one use lost to dice.
   - **No counter-attack.** No enemy on the line counters, whatever its weapon or range.
   - **No element.** Dardan is Dendro, and Dawnbreak applies **no element** – not his, not a tome's if he holds one, not one carried by *Wavelength* – and **triggers no reaction** on an enemy that already carries one; an element lying on a target stays there. The name speaks of daybreak; the strike is **not *Lux***. It is a weapon strike under the Magic System's rule for non-mages, and it neither uses nor spends a Wavelength count (*Interaction*).

   The strike resolves on every target at once: an enemy that falls on the line does not shorten it and does not shield the one behind it.
7. **What it is not.** Not a band ability that reaches another person – the band gives it its length and nothing else; it moves, heals, carries and binds no one. Not a reader of depth – there is no row by rank for it anywhere. Not an affinity source – it earns nothing itself, and a kill made with it counts only as [Affinity](Affinity.md#2--earning-points-deeds-never-proximity) already counts a kill (conservative; *Open decisions → 46*). Not a shield-breaker – it does not wear down a boss's shield, and it is not a strike inside a Chain Attack (conservative; *Open decisions → 47*).

**Breadth, not depth – the design reason.** Every other Nexus ability reads the **depth** of one bond: how far Exchange reaches *this* ally, how much Heartpulse heals *this* unit, how long *this* friend's element lasts, how far apart *these* two may stand. Dawnbreak reads **breadth** – how many people stand with Dardan. A recruit who reached D last chapter and walked up beside him adds exactly as much as the Knight he grew up with at S. That is deliberate: it is the one place in the kit where the width of the roster's bonds, not the height of its favourites, is the resource – the player who opened a rank with everyone, however shallow, strikes further than the player who raised three S-ranks and left the rest at nothing. It is [Pillar 1](../Design-Pillars.md)'s sentence read literally – *your power comes not from yourself alone, but from those who stand beside you* – and it is why the base exists: Dardan alone still strikes, and the strike is short.

**Why the decision the player makes is a real one.** The line wants two things that pull apart: enemies in a row, and friends in a knot. Enemies stand in a row in a corridor, on a bridge, in a column marching at the line – places where a knot of allies around Dardan is either impossible or exactly what the next Enemy Phase punishes. So every use is a small formation puzzle solved against the map's geometry: which friends can close in without exposing themselves, whether an Exchange puts Dardan at the head of the corridor, whether the wall that stops the line cuts off the one enemy worth reaching. And because it is once per chapter, the other question is always *when*: a line fired on turn three to thin the first wave is not there for the boss's escort in the last corridor. Where the bond enters: not in which friend, but in how many – the player who looks at the chart and sees a dozen D-ranks has built Dawnbreak without ever aiming for it.

### 11 – Nexus Mastery

Mastery **upgrades each of the eight abilities individually**. Decided by Dardan, 2026-09-24. It is held from its Part 08 trigger (*Open decisions → 4*) to the end of the game, applies only to abilities Dardan holds, and is locked with the rest of the kit by a black band. **Each ability gets exactly the upgrade below and nothing more**; whatever an upgrade does not name stays as the ability's own section says – every cooldown, every use per chapter, every price. Where an upgrade adds range, the value is the one Mastery lever, the [Nexus Mastery range bonus](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus-mastery), added to the row the base ability reads; no upgrade has a range value of its own.

**Why per ability and not blanket.** The earlier rule – every cooldown removed, two ranges longer – was written over an earlier, smaller kit – before Soulcairn, Wavelength, Bloodoath and Dawnbreak existed. Over eight it either broke abilities that were never meant to lose their cadence (an oath renewed every turn, a permanently borrowed element) or passed them by. An upgrade per ability lets each one grow in its own direction, and keeps the cadence the player learned for fifty chapters intact into the Tower.

#### 11.1 – Exchange

1. **Range.** The Exchange range at every rank is the base row plus the Nexus Mastery range bonus – for the command and for the trigger below alike.
2. **The command is otherwise unchanged.** It keeps its cooldown and stays once per turn (*Open decisions → 3*); *Core Rules → 2* holds in full.
3. **The Enemy-Phase trigger.** On the Enemy Phase, when an enemy's hit on an allied unit **lands and would kill it**, and that ally stands – **at the moment of the blow** – within the Mastery Exchange range for the rank it holds with Dardan, Dardan and the ally **trade places and Dardan takes that hit**:
   - **"Would kill"** is checked after the hit roll and the critical roll, and after every reduction and redirection the ally has – Pavise, a shield, Intercession, a Lifeline split among them (conservative; *Open decisions → 51*). A blow that the ally survives after all of that does not trigger it.
   - **Dardan takes the hit, recalculated.** The rolls stand – the hit landed, and a critical that was rolled is a critical. The damage is computed anew with Dardan as the target: his Def or Res, the terrain of the tile he now stands on, the triangle and effectiveness against his weapon and class (conservative; *Open decisions → 51*). He takes it whole; no Lifeline share applies to it, because the linked ally is no longer the one struck.
   - **It never fires if the recalculated hit would also kill Dardan.** The blow then lands on the ally as rolled. The band does not trade one death for another.
   - **Both landing tiles must be legal**, exactly as for the command (*Core Rules → 2.3*); if either is not, it does not fire.
   - **Automatic.** No prompt, no choice on the Enemy Phase. Whether it will fire is shown before the Enemy Phase begins (*UI & Display*).
   - **Once per unit per map.** Each allied unit can be taken out from under a blow this way at most once per map. A trigger that does not fire spends nothing (conservative; *Open decisions → 52*).
   - **No cooldown.** It neither needs the Exchange cooldown at 0 nor starts it; the command and the trigger are counted apart.
   - **After the swap** both stay on the swapped tiles, and **the enemy's attack ends with the hit Dardan took**: no further strike of that combat – no follow-up, no second hit – and no counter-attack by Dardan or by the ally in it (conservative reading of "ends"; *Open decisions → 51*).
   - **What it does not fire on.** Only an enemy's attack: never on a status tick, a Burning Field or any damage outside a combat, as with Lifeline (*Core Rules → 3*). Not while Dardan cannot act – asleep, paralysed, or under any status that stops a unit acting (conservative; *Open decisions → 52*).

   This is the only place in the kit where Dardan swaps on the Enemy Phase, and the only one where he does it without being asked. It is Ch 01 made a rule – the blow meant for someone else, taken – and it arrives only in the Tower.

#### 11.2 – Lifeline

**Two Lifelines at once**, on two different allies. Each is placed by its own command, under *Core Rules → 3*, and each carries its own share and moves its own ally's statuses onto Dardan; a third is not offered while two stand. **Lifeline range** at every rank is the base row plus the Nexus Mastery range bonus. **Still no floor** – on either line, and on both together. Before Mastery the rule stays one at a time (*Open decisions → 6*).

#### 11.3 – Heartpulse

1. **It cures.** Every unit the pulse reaches – every unit it heals, Dardan included – is freed of **every status effect** it carries, in the sense Lifeline uses the word: what the Status Effects system (backlog) calls a status, and that includes a status Lifeline moved onto Dardan. An **applied element** is not a status (*Open decisions → 10*) and stays; a staff **buff** or **shield** is not an affliction and stays (conservative; *Open decisions → 54*). A unit with no rank is not reached and not cured.
2. **It heals one row higher.** Each unit recovers as if its rank with Dardan were **one higher** in the [Heartpulse table](../Balancing-Guide.md#heartpulse), capped at S: an **S unit** stays on the S row, since there is no row above it; **Dardan himself** stays on his own row – he holds no rank with himself, and the pulse goes outward (conservative; *Open decisions → 54*). A unit with no rank still recovers nothing.
3. **Still once per chapter**, still an action.

#### 11.4 – Earthbound

The seal also closes on **every enemy adjacent to the target** – the four grid neighbours of its tile at the moment the seal is set (conservative; *Open decisions → 55*) – with the **same duration and the same effects, and no roll**. Each carries its own seal counter; the adjacent enemies need not be within Earthbound's range, and the spread does not spread further. The cooldown is unchanged and starts once. **Still "closes, never controls"** (*Core Rules → 5*): nothing about the spread moves, holds or commands anyone.

**Flag for Level 52.** The Dajjal fight (*Core Rules → 6*) must be designed for the Mastery kit: a seal that also closes on the chained units standing beside the Dajjal, and an Exchange that takes the killing blow off an ally on the Enemy Phase. What either does to the chain, the window or the reinforcements is `levelcraft`'s and is not decided here.

#### 11.5 – Soulcairn: the call

Under Mastery Soulcairn gains **one active use**. Dardan named *Fire Emblem Engage*'s Engage state as the inspiration; what is taken is exactly one thing – **the fallen give Dardan something for a limited time**. Nothing else is taken: no stat boost, no signature move, no change of form.

1. **A command, once per map, Classic only.** On his turn, before his action, Dardan calls on the cairn; his movement ends, his action remains. The call needs the pool to be non-empty – no loss, nothing to call on – and a golden band.
2. **Duration.** From the Player Phase of the call, Enemy Phases included, until the start of the Dardan Player Phase the [Soulcairn call duration](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus-mastery) later – counted as turns, the calling turn being the first. It ends early if Dardan falls, and at the end of the map.
3. **The gate lifts – the stacking rule does not.** For the duration, Soulcairn's bonus applies **even when living ranked partners are within the bonus range**. It **never stacks**: of Soulcairn and the best living partner in range, **only the stronger counts**, read by the ordinary rule of [Affinity → 4](Affinity.md#4--the-combat-bonus) – higher rank, then more points, then the order of the affinity list, where Soulcairn stands last (conservative; *Open decisions → 56*). Only Dardan's own bonus is affected; every other unit's is read as always.
4. **The element.** Dardan's ordinary attacks carry **the heaviest fallen unit's element** – read at the moment of the call and fixed for the call (conservative; *Open decisions → 57*) – and **apply it, never trigger it, exactly like Wavelength** (*Core Rules → 8.3, 8.7*): an attack that lands applies it, a miss applies nothing, and there is no count. Combat Arts do what the art says. Dawnbreak carries no element, this one included (*Core Rules → 10.6*).
5. **Wavelength during the call.** If Dardan uses Wavelength while the call runs, the living donor's element **replaces** the fallen element until its count is spent; then the fallen element returns for whatever is left of the call. He still carries one element at a time.
6. **Soulcairn is still no donor.** *Open decisions → 25* stands as it is: the element comes through this upgrade, not by making the cairn a Wavelength donor – so it carries none of Wavelength's rules beyond the two named above, and none of its Mastery upgrade (*→ 11.6*; conservative, *Open decisions → 57*).

**Why losses still do not pay off – under Mastery.** The argument of *Core Rules → 7* holds and gains two sentences. *A living partner of the same rank gives the same bonus every turn*; the call gives it for a few turns once a map, and when a living partner at least as strong is in range the call adds no bonus at all, because only the stronger counts. *A living donor lends the same element* – and more: a Wavelength element replaces the fallen one, and only a Wavelength element spreads under Mastery. The strongest call possible is a cairn built of a deep loss, used on the turns Dardan stands alone – which is the situation the affinity system already punishes, now answered for a few turns rather than for none.

#### 11.6 – Wavelength

Each attack that applies the borrowed element applies it to the target **and to every enemy adjacent to it** – the four grid neighbours of the target's tile (conservative; *Open decisions → 55*). One landing attack still spends **one** from the count, however many enemies it reaches. **Still never triggers**: an adjacent enemy that already carries an element keeps it, as the struck target does (*Core Rules → 8.7*). **Donor range** at every rank is the band's row plus the Nexus Mastery range bonus. Still one element at a time, still on its cooldown.

#### 11.7 – Bloodoath

1. **Act again once per unit.** Each bound unit can be refreshed **once**, by **its own** first Player-Phase kill – a killing blow it lands itself, in its own attack or in its echo strike. The partner's refresh is not spent by it: both bound allies can each be refreshed once per oath. A unit that has been refreshed cannot be refreshed a second time.
2. **Both ranges grow.** The range from Dardan to each bound ally and the pair's **Echo range** are each their base row plus the Nexus Mastery range bonus.
3. **Nothing else moves.** A bound unit's fall still loses the map; there is still no Dual Guard and no defensive half; the oath still cannot be lifted early; the cooldown stays.

#### 11.8 – Dawnbreak

1. **A damage multiplier.** Each strike on the line is multiplied by the [Dawnbreak Mastery multiplier](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus-mastery) – **flat**: it does not grow with the number of counted allies. The value is **not yet set**; until it is, the strike is computed without it (conservative; *Open decisions → 58*).
2. **The counting radius grows.** Allies are counted within the Affinity [bonus range](../Balancing-Guide.md#bonus-range) **plus** the Nexus Mastery range bonus – no radius of its own, still.
3. **Nothing else moves.** The cap is unchanged, the use is still once per chapter, and walls still stop the line.

## Strategic Depth

- **The swap is a position bought with a position.** Exchange pulls the wounded archer out of the press – and puts Dardan into it, unable to step back. Or it puts Dardan on the far side of a wall no one else can cross – and the ally where Dardan stood. Every use is two moves in one, and the second is always Dardan's exposure. The player who reads it as "free rescue" loses Dardan; the player who reads it as "Dardan advances through an ally" finds the aggressive use: swap forward with the flier, attack from the new tile.
- **Rank is reach.** The unit Dardan can reach at range 5 is the unit he has acted for most. That turns the Affinity Chart into a tactical map: whom Dardan heals, swaps, carries and finishes enemies with across the campaign – and whom he chooses to rise with at each chapter's end ([Affinity → Core Rules → 3](Affinity.md#3--rising-the-throttle)) – decides whom he can save from across the room in Part 07. Building the bond is building the range.
- **Lifeline is a bet placed before the dice.** Put it on the unit that has to hold the corridor and Dardan bleeds with him from a safe tile – until the corridor holds too many attackers and the shares add up to Dardan's HP. The forecast shows the share on every enemy attack; the decision is whether to lift it before the Enemy Phase or to trust the numbers. Lifted too early, the ally dies; held too long, Dardan does. And the band carries afflictions whole: a linked healer cannot be silenced or put to sleep, because the sleep lands on Dardan – who then cannot use the Nexus at all until he wakes. The player who links the healer against an enemy with a sleep staff has bought her safety with the whole kit.
- **Heartpulse is one decision per map.** Turn three, when the whole line is scratched, or turn twelve, when three units are one hit from death – and it heals most where the bond is strongest, so the units it saves are the ones the player invested in. Spending it is spending the only map-wide heal the game has.
- **Earthbound picks the enemy that matters.** The healer behind the line, the Sentinel whose Wall of Spears closes the corridor, the boss whose mastery is the whole fight – sealed, each one is a plain fighter for N rounds. It does nothing to a unit that has nothing to lose but a weapon, so the seal is never wasted on a grunt by accident: the forecast lists what will be lost, and "nothing" is a legible answer.
- **Cooldowns are a rhythm, not a stock.** With a flat cooldown, the question is never "should I save it" but "is this the turn". A swap on turn 2 is back on turn 2 + N; the player learns the cadence of the map and places the Exchange where the cadence puts it.
- **Wavelength makes the swordsman a first half of a sentence.** Dardan cannot light a reaction, so the borrowed element is only worth what the roster around him does with it – which turns the question "whose element" into a question about the next two turns and somebody else's action. The Pyro he carries from Hasan is worth nothing until the Hydro Sage fires, and everything the moment she does. It also reaches where the mage cannot: Dardan goes into the press, the element goes with him, and the enemy that was never going to be in a tome's range this turn is carrying Cryo when the line arrives. The price is read before it is paid – a command, a cooldown, his movement – and the count on his blade tells the player exactly how many strikes the setup has left. Where the bond enters: only the friends Dardan is deepest with lend enough attacks to survive an Enemy Phase, so the affinity chart decides not which element he can carry, but how long.
- **Bloodoath stakes the map on a formation.** The Echo pays on every attack either bound unit makes, and the Enemy Phase is where the attacks come without being chosen: a bound front-liner counters every enemy that touches it, and each counter brings the partner's uncounterable strike from a distance the pair's rank allows. That is the whole temptation, and the price sits on the same tile – the unit the enemy attacks most is the unit whose fall loses the battle, and the oath cannot be lifted once the Enemy Phase starts. The play is read before it is committed: the danger overlay counts who can reach the two, the forecast says whether the Echo fires, and the AI does not change its targets because of the oath, so the overlay tells the truth. On the Player Phase it is a sequencing puzzle – Dardan swears first, then the two attack, and the first kill decides who acts twice: aim it with the echo strike and the partner that had already acted moves again. Where the bond enters: Dardan's ranks decide whom he can bind, but the pair's own rank decides how far apart they may stand, so the oath is the one place where the bonds the roster built **without** Dardan feed the Nexus. Lifeline and Exchange are the kit's own insurance, and both must be placed **before** the oath, because both are commands and the oath is the action that ends the turn.
- **Dawnbreak is a formation read twice.** Once for the enemy – where do they stand in a row, and where does a wall cut the row off – and once for the roster: how many ranked friends can stand near Dardan on the turn he strikes, and what does that knot cost on the Enemy Phase that follows. The answers rarely agree, which is the point. The corridor that lines the enemies up is the corridor too narrow for six friends; the open field that fits the friends scatters the enemies. The play the ability rewards is the approach: bringing allies up over two turns so that the line is long on the turn the enemy column arrives, or using an Exchange to put Dardan at the head of the row from a tile the knot already surrounds. It has no rank to grow into, so the chart feeds it in a different way from the rest of the kit – every stranger turned into a D-rank is one tile, and a player who spent fifty chapters making sure nobody on the roster is a stranger to Dardan has a longer line than one who made three people his whole world. And because it is once per chapter, holding it is a real option: the line fired at the first wave is the line not there for the escort in the last corridor.
- **Soulcairn changes no plan – it changes what a loss leaves.** Outside the Mastery call (*Core Rules → 11.5*) there is no play built around it: a player who wants the bonus wants a living partner beside Dardan, and that is the play the affinity system already asks for. What it does is make the aftermath legible. The chapter after a death, Dardan's list carries a row that says what that person had been worth to him, in the game's own units; and on the map where the front line breaks and Dardan stands alone, the forecast names the friend who is not there. The tactical content is a single reading: *if nobody living is in range, the bonus is the cairn's, and it is exactly as strong as the bond that was lost.* A player at the Tower with a full roster never sees it fire; a player who arrives with half of one sees it every time the line thins – and would still rather have the half back.
- **Mastery keeps the rhythm and changes the questions.** Nothing comes off cooldown, so the Tower is played in the cadence the player has learned – but each ability asks something new. The Enemy-Phase Exchange turns the danger overlay into a ledger: which allies still have their one rescue, and where Dardan must stand so that the ones who do are inside his range and the blow they would take would not kill him too – a Dardan at low HP rescues nobody. Two Lifelines are two bets on one purse. The cleansing pulse answers the status-heavy map, and is still the only one of the chapter. A seal on the healer who stands beside the bodyguard closes both; a borrowed element set into a knot sets the whole knot. A doubly refreshed oath rewards ordering both kills. The Soulcairn call is a timing decision in a kit that had none for the dead: the few turns Dardan will stand alone. And Dawnbreak counts from further away, so the knot it needs no longer has to be a knot.
- **The Dajjal is the thesis.** Every chained unit heals him; every seal opens a window. The fight is not won by the biggest number on the roster but by the timing of the one ability that runs on trust – and by the bonds that give Exchange and Heartpulse their reach inside the window.

## Design Pillars

| Pillar | Question | Answer |
| ------ | -------- | ------ |
| **Bonds** | Does it strengthen the connections between units? | Yes – it is the pillar's first gameplay expression by name ([Design Pillars](../Design-Pillars.md)). Six of the eight abilities read the affinity rank between Dardan and living units, so the bond is not flavour on the mechanic, it is the range and the strength of it – with *Wavelength* the bond decides what his blade carries and for how long, with *Bloodoath* the bond two of his friends hold with each other decides how the oath between them can be used, and with *Dawnbreak* the number of friends near him who hold any rank at all decides how far his strike reaches. A seventh, Earthbound, exists to sever a bond that was turned into a chain; the eighth, Soulcairn, keeps a bond the player can no longer act on. The system says: trust reaches further than control. |
| **Depth** | Easy to learn, hard to master? | Yes. "Swap places", "share the damage", "heal everyone once", "seal one enemy", "carry a friend's element", "bind two friends to each other", "strike a line as long as the friends beside you" are each one sentence, and Ch 08 teaches the first with one hostage. Mastering it is reading rank-ranges across fourteen deployed units, deciding whose burden Dardan carries on the Enemy Phase, timing four cooldowns against the map's cadence, holding one pulse for the right turn – knowing which element the next two turns will need, given that Dardan can set one but never light it, and which two units can hold where the enemy will come. |
| **Weight** | Do the decisions have long-term consequences? | Yes. Lifeline can kill Dardan – permanently, in Classic. Heartpulse and Dawnbreak are each spent for the chapter. Exchange leaves Dardan where he cannot walk away from. Bloodoath puts the map on two lives and cannot be taken back. And the reach of every ability is the sum of affinity decisions made over fifty chapters, which cannot be re-rolled. |
| **Integration** | Does the mechanic tell a story? | Yes, more than any other. The swap of Ch 01 is Dardan's essence – he takes the blow meant for Leona – and the player performs it in Ch 08 for Elena. Heartpulse is born in Ch 21's darkness. The seal is Aurevia's own act, learned in the trial of Ch 43 and set on the Dajjal in Ch 52. The band's colour on the HUD is the state of the story. |
| **Fairness** | Is the challenge respectful of the player's time? | Yes, by construction: every cost is visible before use (cooldowns, the chapter use, the band's colour, the Lifeline share in the forecast, the list of what Earthbound will remove), no Nexus ability rolls to hit, a locked chapter shows the lock from turn one, and the Ch 05 scene keeps the Exchange out of the player's hands so the level never asks the player to have been trapped by a rule he had. A player who loses Dardan to Lifeline saw the share on every forecast. |

**Anti-pillar check:** No grinding – nothing in the Nexus is fed by kills or levels; it is fed by affinity ranks, which [Affinity](Affinity.md) keeps ungrindable with its one-rank-per-chapter throttle and its refusal to pay for proximity. No power without price – position (Exchange), HP with no floor (Lifeline), the only use of the chapter (Heartpulse), a full action and a cooldown (Earthbound), a command, a cooldown and a counted handful of strikes that cannot finish what they start (Wavelength), a full action, a cooldown and the map itself (Bloodoath), a full action, the chapter's one use and a knot of allies the Enemy Phase will find (Dawnbreak). Nothing that works only with over-trained units – the Nexus reads rank, not stats, so an under-levelled Dardan has the same reach as a capped one. Nothing generic – no other game's Lord has it, and it is the name on the box.

**Soulcairn under the same five questions**, run separately because it is the one ability that does not ask the player for a decision (*Core Rules → 7*):

| Pillar | Answer for Soulcairn |
| ------ | -------------------- |
| **Bonds** | Yes. It is the affinity bond, continued past the one thing that otherwise ends it. The pillar's own wording – *the loss of a unit means more because it was part of a social fabric* – is what the rule measures: the fallen unit's weight on the band is its frozen points, and the design says that a bond does not stop existing because one end of it did. It strengthens no living connection, and it should not; it is the system's answer to the pillar's third question, *how does the player feel when he loses this unit*. |
| **Depth** | Yes, at the low end by intent. "The dead stay on Dardan's list as one partner; they help only when nobody living is near" is ten seconds. There is little to master, and the little there is – knowing that a lone Dardan is not quite alone, and how much – is the right amount for a rule whose whole content is *fill the gap, never stack*. |
| **Weight** | Yes – it is *made of* weight. Every point in the pool is a decision the player cannot take back; the pool only grows by the one irreversible event the game has. It adds no new consequence of its own and removes none: a death still costs the unit, its deeds, its rank-ups, its Heart-to-Hearts and its bonus from a tile. Soulcairn returns the last of those, in the weakest form, and nothing else. |
| **Integration** | Yes. The Nexus is "das Band zwischen den Seelen" (Ch 29) and the Affinity Chart is that band drawn; a link that goes grey but does not vanish is the chart saying what the story says – that the band remembers. The row named *Soulcairn*, the greyed links running into it, and a forecast that names the fallen friend when Dardan fights alone are the mechanic telling the story without a line of dialogue. |
| **Fairness** | Yes, by construction. The rank, points and element are on the list at all times; the forecast names the partner when it applies; the gate ("no living ranked partner within range") is the same distance rule the player already reads for every living partner. There is nothing to learn after the fact and nothing to optimise, so nothing to get wrong. And the ability can never punish: it only ever adds, and only where the player would otherwise have had nothing. |

**Wavelength under the same five questions**, run separately because it is the one ability that reaches into a second system (*Core Rules → 8*):

| Pillar | Answer for Wavelength |
| ------ | --------------------- |
| **Bonds** | Yes, and in the most literal form the kit has: what crosses the band is the thing [Affinity](Affinity.md#7--elemental-affinity-and-who-has-none) says a person *brings* to a bond. The bond decides who can lend (rank D and a living ally in rank-range) and how long the loan lasts (attacks by rank). And because Dardan can never trigger the reaction himself, every use ends in somebody else's hands – the ability is cooperative by construction, not by encouragement. |
| **Depth** | Yes. "Borrow an ally's element for a few swings" is ten seconds. Mastering it is the elemental table, the reaction table, the enemy's weaknesses and two turns of foresight at once – which element, from whom, onto which enemy, for whom to light. It is the only place where the magic system and the bond system have to be read together. |
| **Weight** | Partly – and deliberately so. A single use is a turn's decision, not a campaign's: the element is spent in a few strikes and the cooldown returns. The long-term weight sits where the kit always puts it, in the affinity ranks that decide how many attacks each friend's element buys – choices made over fifty chapters that cannot be re-rolled. Nothing about Wavelength is irreversible, and nothing about it needs to be. |
| **Integration** | Yes. The band is what the Guru describes in Ch 29 – "die unsichtbare Verbindung … zwischen allen lebenden Wesen" – and this is that line running the other way: for once it is Dardan who receives. A Lord who cannot cast but can carry what his friends are is the story's own sentence about him, and the one exception written into the Magic System is exactly the size of that sentence. |
| **Fairness** | Yes. The donor list, the element each lends and the number of attacks are shown before the command is confirmed; the carried element and the remaining count sit on Dardan while it runs; and every forecast says which element the strike applies **and** that it will not trigger a reaction – the one thing a player could otherwise expect wrongly. No hit roll is added, nothing is hidden, and a misread costs a cooldown, not a unit. |

**Anti-pillar check for Wavelength:** No grinding – the count is bought with affinity rank, and Affinity's throttle bounds that. No power without price – a command, the movement, a cooldown, and a hard ceiling on what the power *is*: applying without triggering. Nothing that works only with over-trained units – it reads rank and the donor's sheet, never stats. Nothing generic – no Lord in the genre borrows an ally's element with a sword. **The trap check:** the combination that could trivialise a fight would be *apply and ignite in one unit*, and that is the exact thing rule 7 forbids; the second candidate, Exchange into the press plus a borrowed element in the same turn, spends two cooldowns and still buys one attack from one unit.

**Bloodoath under the same five questions**, run separately because it is the one ability that acts on a bond Dardan is not part of, and the only one that can lose the map without Dardan falling (*Core Rules → 9*):

| Pillar | Answer for Bloodoath |
| ------ | -------------------- |
| **Bonds** | Yes, and in a direction the kit had not used: the oath needs a rank **between the two allies**, and that rank sets how far apart they may stand and still strike together. The bonds the player built across the roster – two Knights who healed and rescued each other for twenty chapters – become something the Nexus reads. And the rule states the pillar's own question outright: for one Enemy Phase, the two are one fate – either one's fall is everyone's. |
| **Depth** | Yes. "Two friends swear; each strikes where the other strikes; the first kill acts again; if either falls, the battle is lost" is ten seconds. Mastering it is reading two range tables at once (Dardan to each, the pair to each other), predicting where the Enemy Phase will land, ordering a Player Phase so the first kill refreshes the right unit, and deciding whether Lifeline or Exchange goes in front of the oath this turn. |
| **Weight** | Partly – deliberately, like Wavelength. A single oath is a turn's decision: it lasts one Enemy Phase and its cooldown returns. The stake inside that turn is the heaviest the kit has short of Dardan's own life – the map – but in Classic the reset means it never costs a unit permanently. The long-term weight sits in the affinity ranks: whom Dardan can bind, and which pairs of friends can stand far enough apart to be worth binding, are fifty chapters of choices. |
| **Integration** | Partly, and pending the chapter. The band is "das Band zwischen den Seelen" (Ch 29), and an oath is that band made binding – a promise that two people carry each other's fate. The rule itself tells that story without a line of text: the reward is striking together, the price is falling together. How and where the story first shows an oath across the band is a chapter decision that has not been made (*Open decisions → 39*); until then the integration rests on the band alone, which is honest but not yet the story's. |
| **Fairness** | Yes, by construction, and it has to be, because the price is the map. The defeat condition is shown **before** the oath is confirmed and stands in the objective display for as long as it holds; the Echo has no roll; every forecast says whether the Echo fires and, if not, why; the enemy AI does not change its targets, so the danger overlay the player already reads tells the truth about the risk. A player who loses a map to the oath saw the condition on screen, saw the enemies who could reach the bound unit, and chose to swear anyway. |

**Anti-pillar check for Bloodoath:** No grinding – it reads ranks and earns nothing by being sworn. No power without price – a full action, a cooldown, and a defeat condition that cannot be lifted once the Enemy Phase shows what it holds. Nothing that works only with over-trained units – it reads ranks, not stats; the echo strike is only as strong as the partner already is. Nothing generic – Dual Strike exists in the genre, but not guaranteed, not at a distance set by a bond between two allies, and not tied to a shared defeat.

**The trap check.** *Is it ever the right answer, given the price?* Yes, in three situations: an Enemy Phase in which one sturdy unit will take several attacks and a partner can stand within the pair's range out of reach – every counter then carries a second, uncounterable strike; a Player Phase in which one more kill would open the map and a second turn for the right unit is worth the risk; and a map in which the two are both safe and the extra strikes cost nothing but the action. *Is it ever wrong?* Yes – whenever either unit can fall, and the overlay and the forecast say so before the oath is sworn. That is the point: the option is right or wrong for reasons visible on the screen, never by trap. *Is there a combination that trivialises a fight?* The candidates, and why they stop: **Echo on Echo** – forbidden, the echo strike triggers nothing (*→ 4*). **Galeforce chains** – once per oath, spent at the first kill, never on the Enemy Phase. **Echo as a shield-breaker** – forbidden, the echo strike does not wear the shield (*→ 4*). **The bound wall on the Enemy Phase** is the strongest honest use – a counter plus an echo on every enemy that attacks – and it is bounded by one Enemy Phase per cooldown, the pair's range, and the fact that the unit taking every attack is the unit whose fall loses the map. **Lifeline on the bound wall** moves part of that risk onto Dardan, whose fall also loses the map – it spreads the stake over three lives rather than removing it. **Nexus Mastery** would have been the one trivialising case under the old blanket rule (an oath renewed every turn); the per-ability upgrade keeps the cooldown and gives the oath a second refresh and longer ranges instead (*Core Rules → 11.7*) – two second turns per oath at most, still one Enemy Phase per cooldown, still the map at stake. **The Enemy-Phase Exchange on a bound unit** is the Mastery kit's strongest insurance for the oath: it can take a killing blow off a bound ally – once per ally per map, never if the blow would kill Dardan, whose fall also loses the map.

**Dawnbreak under the same five questions**, run separately because it is the one ability that reads the breadth of Dardan's bonds rather than the depth of one, and the only one that strikes (*Core Rules → 10*):

| Pillar | Answer for Dawnbreak |
| ------ | -------------------- |
| **Bonds** | Yes, and it is the pillar's own sentence made into a rule: *your power comes not from yourself alone, but from those who stand beside you.* The length is the number of friends standing beside him. It strengthens the bonds in a direction the rest of the kit does not: where every other ability rewards raising a few pairs high, Dawnbreak rewards opening a rank with everyone – a stranger on the roster is a tile the line never gets. |
| **Depth** | Yes. "Once a map, Dardan strikes in a line; every friend near him makes it one tile longer; walls stop it; it never hurts allies" is ten seconds. Mastering it is the geometry of two formations at once – the enemies' row and the allies' knot – and the timing of a single use against a whole map. |
| **Weight** | Partly – deliberately, like Wavelength and Bloodoath. A single use is a chapter's decision, spent and gone for that map; nothing about it is irreversible beyond the map. The long-term weight sits where the kit puts it: the count reads ranks built over fifty chapters, and whom the player never bonded with is a tile he can never add. |
| **Integration** | Partly, and pending the chapter. The strike is what the band has always been in the story – the people Dardan trusts, and his strength being theirs – and the rules tell it without text: alone he still strikes, short; with his friends around him the line crosses the room. How and where the story first shows it is a chapter decision that has not been made (*Open decisions → 48*); until then the integration rests on the band alone. |
| **Fairness** | Yes, by construction. The count, the counted allies, the ones not counted and why, the line in every direction, the wall that stops it and every enemy it strikes with the damage each takes are shown before it is confirmed; nothing rolls; no enemy counters; allies cannot be hurt by it, so a misread line costs the chapter's use, never a unit. |

**Anti-pillar check for Dawnbreak:** No grinding – the count reads whether ranks exist, and D costs one deed, which Affinity's own sources pay without any farming; no rank is worth more to it than D, so there is nothing to push. No power without price – a full action, the chapter's one use, Dardan's tile at the head of the line and a knot of allies standing where the next Enemy Phase finds them. Nothing that works only with over-trained units – it reads the existence of ranks, not stats; its damage is only Dardan's ordinary strike. Nothing generic – line attacks exist in the genre, but not one whose length is counted in the hero's friends.

**The trap check.** *Is it ever the right answer?* Yes: a corridor, a bridge or a column where three or more enemies stand in a row and Dardan's ordinary attack would reach one; a finishing turn in which several wounded enemies line up; a moment when the enemy is walled off except for one lane. *Is it ever wrong?* Yes – when the knot of allies it needs would be caught by the Enemy Phase, and when the map still holds a better row later; both are visible on the screen, the danger overlay for the first and the map for the second. *Is holding it a trap?* No – unused, it costs nothing, and the preview tells the player at every turn exactly what it would do. *Is there a combination that trivialises a fight?* The candidates, and why they stop: **Dawnbreak and Heartpulse** – both once per chapter, both actions, never the same turn. **Exchange into the head of a corridor** – the strongest honest use, and it stops at the cap, at the first wall and at the one use per chapter; the knot still has to be around Dardan's new tile, which is the ally's old tile. **Wavelength before the strike** – nothing: Dawnbreak carries no element and spends no count. **A shield opener** – forbidden in the conservative reading: it wears down no boss's shield (*Open decisions → 47*). **Nexus Mastery** – a flat multiplier and a wider counting radius (*Core Rules → 11.8*); the cap, the one use and the walls are untouched, and the multiplier does not grow with the count, so it strengthens the strike without making the formation worth more than the cap allows. The one real ceiling is the cap, which is why it is a rule Dardan set rather than a lever.

**Anti-pillar check for Soulcairn:** No grinding – the pool cannot be fed by anything the player does on purpose except losing units, and the thresholds make many small losses worth less than one real bond. No power without price – the price is the unit, paid in full and before the ability exists; and the return is bounded by the price (*Core Rules → 7*, the argument). Nothing that works only with over-trained units – it reads frozen affinity points, not stats. Nothing generic – no game in the genre keeps the dead on the support chart as a partner. The specific trap this ability could have been – *losses become desirable* – is closed three ways in *Core Rules → 7* and is the reason *Circle* and *Watch* were rejected. The Mastery call does not reopen it (*Core Rules → 11.5*, the argument).

**Nexus Mastery under the same five questions**, run for the rework of 2026-09-24 (*Core Rules → 11*):

| Pillar | Answer for Nexus Mastery |
| ------ | ------------------------ |
| **Bonds** | Yes. Every range upgrade adds to a row the bond already sets, so rank still orders the roster in the Tower. The Enemy-Phase Exchange is the band answering for a friend without being asked, once each; the Soulcairn call lets the fallen stand with Dardan beside the living rather than only in their absence – and never above the stronger of the two. |
| **Depth** | Yes. Each upgrade is one sentence on top of an ability the player has used for chapters – "he takes the killing blow once per friend", "two lines", "the pulse cleanses", "the seal spreads to its neighbours", "once a map, the fallen stand with him for a few turns" – and nothing about the base abilities has to be relearned, because no cadence changes. |
| **Weight** | Yes. Nothing that cost something stops costing it: every cooldown and use per chapter stays. The Enemy-Phase rescue is spent once per ally per map and moves Dardan under the blow; the second Lifeline doubles an uncapped risk; the call exists only because someone was lost. |
| **Integration** | Yes. The Tower is where the band reaches furthest, and the upgrades say what the story says about Dardan at the end: he takes the blow meant for someone else, as in Ch 01 – now as a rule; the dead stand with him; the seal Aurevia set on the Nexus-Seers closes on more than one. The in-world moment of the unlock waits on its trigger (*Open decisions → 4*). |
| **Fairness** | Yes, if the display carries it. The rescue is automatic, so the player must see before the Enemy Phase which allies still have it and whether it would fire – and the rescue never kills Dardan, so it cannot turn a saved ally into a lost Lord. The call shows its turns remaining; every upgraded preview (two lines, the cure list, the adjacent seals, the adjacent element targets, the wider count, the multiplied damage) is shown before commitment (*UI & Display*). No roll is added anywhere. |

**Anti-pillar check for Nexus Mastery:** No grinding – it unlocks at a Part 08 trigger and reads the same ranks as before. No power without price – it removes no cost; each addition carries its own limit (once per ally per map, once per map, the same cooldowns). Nothing that works only with over-trained units – it reads rank and position, never stats. **The trap check:** *Enemy-Phase Exchange plus Lifeline* – the recalculated blow lands on Dardan whole, and it never fires if it would kill him, so the combination cannot kill him through the rescue; it spends his HP, which the Lifeline shares also spend. *Two Lifelines plus the rescue* – each line and each rescue is bounded, and all draw on one HP bar with no floor. *Spreading seal plus spreading element* – two different actions and a command on separate cooldowns; a knot of enemies sealed and set is the reward for having found the knot. *The call plus Wavelength* – one element at a time, the borrowed replacing the fallen. Nothing here removes a turn limit, which is what the old blanket rule did.

## Introduction

**First chapter:** **Ch 08** for the system (Exchange); **Ch 21** for Heartpulse; **Ch 44** for Earthbound; Lifeline, Wavelength, Bloodoath and Dawnbreak *open*; Soulcairn has **no chapter** – it appears at the first Classic loss, wherever that falls. Ch 01 and Ch 05 show the swap in prose before the player has it.

**How it is introduced:** In four situations, each of which makes the new ability the obvious answer – and one that is not a situation at all.

- *Ch 01, Every End.* The child Dardan, without knowing what he does, stands where Leona stood and takes the sword. The player sees the swap once, involuntary, and pays for it with the whole first chapter's weight. No rule yet.
- *Ch 05, A Heart of Gold.* The first conscious swap – Dardan calls the memory of Ch 01 back and trades places with Leona through the Guardian's barrier. The player sees that it goes through walls. **Level 05 does not offer the Exchange**: Dardan is meant to be trapped inside the barrier with the Guardian, and a swap out would destroy the scene the level is built on. The chapter's level box and the Ch 08 prose were aligned to this on 2026-09-20.
- *Ch 08, The Vigilant Knights.* The cult leader takes Elena hostage with a blade at her throat; the level begins; the player must perform the Exchange with Elena himself. The tutorial is the hostage: there is one ally the band can reach, one tile the player wants Dardan on, and a wall of cultists in between that the band ignores. The player learns range-through-everything, the position price (Dardan is now next to the man with the knife) and the cooldown – all from one swap. **Elena is reachable because, for Level 08 only, she is a unit of the player's faction** – forced deployment, that one map – **and she can only heal**: a staff user with no attack. Decided by Dardan; the "own units only" rule stands unchanged. She joins the roster properly in Ch 25 as her sheet says. Her battle-local data for that map – class as deployed, staff, starting position – is `levelcraft`'s and belongs in the Level 08 document, not here and not on her sheet.
- *Ch 21, Seek the Light Within.* Dardan and Tifa in the centre of the labyrinth, wounded, the other pairs scattered in the dark corridors. Heartpulse is born the turn before the Guardian strikes, and the level box says it is usable from here, once per chapter. The map teaches it by shape: the pairs are too far apart for any staff, and one pulse reaches all of them.
- *Ch 43 – Ch 44, She Who Judges All / …Must Face the One Who Refuses to Fall.* The trial opens Dardan's access to the Nexus; Varnel names the seal; in Ch 44 Dardan says he can set it. Level 44 is the first map on which an enemy can be sealed – the level should put one enemy on the map whose abilities are the problem (a healer, an aura, a caster), so that the seal is the obvious answer.
- *Lifeline –* no chapter. Until Dardan sets one, no map carries it and no level box announces it.
- *Wavelength –* no chapter, for the same reason and in the same state. **This document proposes none**, because the choice is a story decision: the map that introduces it has to show an ally lending Dardan something across the band, and which ally and which chapter that is belongs to the chapters, not to the spec. What the level will have to carry when Dardan does set it, so that the map teaches it without a popup: an enemy whose weakness is an element Dardan does not have, an ally in rank-range who has it, and someone able to light what he sets – the reaction is the lesson, and it needs two units. Until then Wavelength is a proposal in `mechanicraft`'s sense and appears in no level's *New Mechanics* column (*Open decisions → 31*).
- *Bloodoath –* no chapter, in the same state as Lifeline and Wavelength. **This document proposes none**: which two allies first swear, and in which chapter the band first binds two people to each other, is a story decision. What the level will have to carry when Dardan does set it, so that the map teaches it without a popup: two allies who already hold a rank with each other, a position where one of them will clearly draw the Enemy Phase while the other can stand within the pair's range out of reach – the counter-and-echo is the lesson – and an enemy the pair can kill on the Player Phase, so the act-again is seen once. Because the price is the map, the introducing level must not make the oath the **only** way through: the player learns the defeat condition by reading it, and a first map that forced the risk would teach the wrong lesson. Until then Bloodoath is a proposal in `mechanicraft`'s sense and appears in no level's *New Mechanics* column (*Open decisions → 39*).
- *Dawnbreak –* no chapter, in the same state as Lifeline, Wavelength and Bloodoath. **This document proposes none**: in which chapter Dardan first strikes with his friends' strength, and who stands with him when he does, is a story decision. What the level will have to carry when Dardan does set it, so that the map teaches it without a popup: a **row of enemies** – a corridor, a bridge, a column advancing on one lane – that Dardan's ordinary attack cannot answer; **allies holding a rank with Dardan who can close in around him** over a turn or two, so that the player sees the counted line grow in the preview as each one arrives – the growth is the lesson, and it needs the player to see it change; and **one wall** across a second lane, so that the line visibly stops where the rest of the kit never would. Because it is once per chapter, the introducing map should not require it to be fired early: the player learns the count by watching it, and a first map that forced the strike on turn one would teach the length but not the breadth. Until then Dawnbreak is a proposal in `mechanicraft`'s sense and appears in no level's *New Mechanics* column (*Open decisions → 48*).
- *Nexus Mastery – Part 08, trigger open* (*Open decisions → 4*). **This document proposes no chapter and no scene.** What the first Mastery map will have to carry, so that the upgrades are found rather than explained: an Enemy Phase in which one ally inside Dardan's range will take a blow that kills it, with the rescue marker visible beforehand – the player learns the trigger by watching it fire once; and enemies standing in knots, so the spreading seal and the spreading element show their reach in the preview. The Soulcairn call needs no map – it exists only in a Classic run with losses, and its command simply appears on Dardan.
- *Soulcairn – the first death, in whichever chapter Classic first allows one.* Decided: no scripted scene, no level box, no tutorial map, no chapter gate – a mechanic that only exists when the player has lost someone cannot be scheduled, and a map built to teach it would be a map built to kill a unit. **The form of the in-world moment is open** and is Dardan's (*Open decisions → 20*). The **proposal**, in its most conservative form: nothing but the new row. When the chapter ends and the between-chapter selection opens, Dardan's affinity list carries a row named *Soulcairn* it did not carry before, with a rank, points and the fallen unit's element, and on the Affinity Chart the greyed link of the fallen runs into a new node. No line of dialogue, no popup; the first time the bonus applies, the forecast names the fallen unit, and the player looks for why – the same way Ch 06 lets him find the Affinity line. Anything more – a short map talk, a line from Dardan at the cairn – is Lorekeeper's to write and Dardan's to decide; this document does not propose text.

**What the player must already know:** Movement and terrain by Move Type (Ch 01, Ch 03), that abilities and commands are different things (Base masteries, Ch 06 onward), the battle forecast (Ch 01), staves and healing (Ch 01, the Acolyte line) before Heartpulse, and the ability catalog's vocabulary of what an enemy can do – auras, arts, staves – before Earthbound means anything.

**Level index.** Ch 08 carries *The Nexus / Exchange* in `design/levels/README.md`; Ch 07 carries the first chapter played in the chosen classes, as the [Progression System](../Progression-System.md#part-01-path-of-liberation-chapters-1-8) already said. The *New Mechanics* column exists only for Part 01; Ch 21 and Ch 44 are annotated in their status cells. The [Progression System](../Progression-System.md#part-03-the-champions-road-chapters-17-24) carries Heartpulse at Ch 21 and the desert with Rohan at Ch 22, matching the chapters.

| Bloodoath Echo range by the pair's rank (D–S) | How far apart the two bound allies may stand and still strike together – the lever that decides whether a pair can hold a wall and a firing line at once |
| Bloodoath echo strike strength | How much the partner's strike is worth relative to its ordinary attack; not decided – conservatively a full ordinary strike (*Open decisions → 34*) |
| Dawnbreak base length, step per counted ally, cap | Set by Dardan as **rules** and recorded in the [Balancing Guide → Dawnbreak](../Balancing-Guide.md#dawnbreak) – not levers. Listed here so that nobody tunes them by accident |
| Dawnbreak strike strength | How much each strike on the line is worth relative to Dardan's ordinary strike with his equipped weapon; not decided – conservatively the ordinary strike (*Open decisions → 41*) |
| Dawnbreak count radius | **No value of its own.** The allies are counted within the Affinity [bonus range](../Balancing-Guide.md#bonus-range); tuning that range tunes Dawnbreak with it |
| *Soulcairn* | **No value of its own.** It reads the Affinity [rank thresholds](../Balancing-Guide.md#rank-thresholds), [combat bonus by rank](../Balancing-Guide.md#combat-bonus-by-rank), [element mixes](../Balancing-Guide.md#element-mixes) and [bonus range](../Balancing-Guide.md#bonus-range) as they stand. Tuning any of those tunes Soulcairn with them; there is no separate lever, by decision |

**Rules, not tuning values**, and therefore here: the cooldown is flat and does not scale with rank; Heartpulse is once per chapter; Lifeline has no floor and moves statuses whole; Earthbound has no hit roll and no rank scaling; range goes through everything; one Lifeline at a time (two under Mastery); Nexus Mastery upgrades each ability individually, removes no cooldown and no use per chapter, and adds its range bonus only where an upgrade names it; the Enemy-Phase Exchange is automatic, once per unit per map, off the cooldown and never fires if the recalculated hit would kill Dardan; the Soulcairn call is a command once per map; Soulcairn sums frozen points, is one partner, is capped at S, fills the gap and never stacks, carries the heaviest fallen unit's element, and exists in Classic only; Wavelength is a command on a cooldown rather than a use per chapter, borrows the element and nothing else, fixes it at the moment of the command, applies without a Combat Art as the Magic System's one exception, and never triggers a reaction – and its attack count rises monotonically with the rank, whatever the row says; Bloodoath is an action on a flat cooldown, binds two living allies who each hold a rank with Dardan and one with each other, lasts exactly until Dardan's next Player Phase and cannot be lifted early, fires its Echo without a roll whenever the partner is in the pair's range at that moment, its echo strike cannot be countered, wears down no shield and triggers no further Echo, its act-again fires once per oath on the first Player-Phase kill, and either bound unit's fall loses the map; Dawnbreak is an action once per chapter, counts every living ranked ally within the bonus range as one whatever the rank – never a beast, an Other-faction unit or Soulcairn – adds one equal step per counted ally to its base up to its cap, runs straight from Dardan's tile, is stopped by walls and by nothing else, strikes every enemy on the line and no one else, draws no counter and applies no element – and, in the conservative reading, rolls nothing (*Open decisions → 44*).

## Interaction with Other Mechanics

| Mechanic | Interaction |
| -------- | ----------- |
| **[Affinity](Affinity.md)** | The dependency, in both directions. Exchange range, Lifeline range and Heartpulse strength read the affinity rank between Dardan and the unit as the pair holds it when the map begins; the [starting ranks](../Balancing-Guide.md#starting-ranks) put the eight Vigilant Knights at C with Dardan and Elena at B in Ch 08. In return Exchange, Lifeline and Heartpulse are affinity *sources* for Dardan and the unit they touch – values in the [Balancing Guide → Points per source](../Balancing-Guide.md#points-per-source); Earthbound is not. Affinity's own *combat bonus* (the *Affinity Bonus* in the [formulas](../Balancing-Guide.md#-damage-calculation-formula), from the strongest partner within the bonus range) is a separate effect and is touched by exactly one thing here: **Soulcairn** is a partner on Dardan's list – the fallen, summed – that gives that bonus for its rank only when no living ranked partner is within the bonus range (*Core Rules → 7*). The strongest-partner rule is unchanged; Soulcairn is always in range and always loses to any living partner. It is fed by no source and feeds none. **Wavelength** reads the rank twice – for who may lend (any rank from D, within rank-range) and for how many attacks the loan lasts – and feeds the bond **not at all**: it is no affinity source, because the donor performs no deed (*Core Rules → 8.8*). What it borrows is the donor's **elemental affinity**; the pair's own *combat bonus* is untouched and still reads both units' element mixes as always. **Bloodoath** reads three ranks – Dardan's with each of the two, for whom he can bind from where, and **the two allies' rank with each other**, which must exist and which sets their Echo range: the first Nexus rule that reads a pair Dardan is not in. All three are read as they stood when the map began. It is **no affinity source** in its own right: swearing it earns nothing – the two have not yet done anything for each other when the oath is sworn. What they do under it counts exactly as Affinity already counts any attack, no more: an echo strike is an attack by the partner, so a target the one damages and the other kills is an ordinary **shared kill** ([Affinity → 2](Affinity.md#2--earning-points-deeds-never-proximity)) for the pair – the oath makes such kills likely, it does not pay extra for them (conservative; *Open decisions → 36*). **Dawnbreak** reads the rank in a way no other ability does: only **whether** a unit near Dardan holds one – any rank from D, as held when the map began – and **how many** do; the depth of each is ignored. It also reads one value of Affinity's own, the [bonus range](../Balancing-Guide.md#bonus-range), as the radius in which it counts; that range is Affinity's lever and Dawnbreak owns none of its own. It is **no affinity source**: the strike earns nothing by being struck, and a kill made with it counts only as Affinity already counts a kill – an enemy an ally damaged earlier in the round and Dawnbreak finishes is an ordinary shared kill for that pair (conservative; *Open decisions → 46*). **Under Nexus Mastery** (*Core Rules → 11*): the Enemy-Phase Exchange is an Exchange and pays the existing Exchange source, no more (*Open decisions → 53*); the cleansing Heartpulse pays only its existing Heartpulse source, not a separate *Help – cure* (*Open decisions → 54*); the Soulcairn call lifts Soulcairn's gate for its duration but not the rule that only the strongest partner counts, and it feeds nothing; the other upgrades add no source. |
| **[Affinity](Affinity.md) – Skill Links, rejected** | Wavelength is **not** a Skill Link, and it is the reason that rejection is worth restating ([Affinity → What Affinity is not](Affinity.md#what-affinity-is-not-skill-links-rejected)). What XC1 lent between characters were *abilities*, which in this game are the mechanical translation of who a person is – Leona with Hasan's ability is less Leona. Wavelength lends the **element**, the one property Affinity already calls *what a unit brings to a bond*, and nothing else: no ability, art, stat, growth, weapon rank or class trait crosses the band. Widening that boundary would reopen the rejection; keeping it is what lets both stand. |
| **Nexus Mastery** (Lord Kit) | Upgrades each of the eight individually (*Core Rules → 11*) and removes **no** cooldown and **no** use per chapter. Exchange: range bonus, plus the automatic Enemy-Phase trigger – once per unit per map, off the cooldown, never if the recalculated hit would kill Dardan. Lifeline: two at once, range bonus, no floor. Heartpulse: cures every status and heals one row higher, still once per chapter. Earthbound: the seal spreads to every adjacent enemy, same duration, no roll. Soulcairn: a once-per-map command – the bonus applies beside living partners (only the stronger counts) and Dardan's attacks apply the heaviest fallen unit's element. Wavelength: the element also lands on every enemy adjacent to the target; donor range bonus. Bloodoath: act-again once per unit; both ranges get the bonus. Dawnbreak: a flat damage multiplier (value not set) and a counting radius of bonus range plus range bonus; cap, use and walls unchanged. |
| **Mastery: Enemy-Phase Exchange ↔ Lifeline** | "Would kill" is read after the Lifeline split on the linked ally (*Core Rules → 11.1*): if the ally survives its remainder, nothing fires. If it fires, Dardan takes the recalculated hit whole – no share applies, since the ally is not struck. Both draw on Dardan's HP with no floor; the rescue itself never kills him. |
| **Mastery: Enemy-Phase Exchange ↔ Bloodoath** | A bound unit can be taken out from under a killing blow – once per unit per map – which keeps the oath's defeat condition from firing. Dardan's fall still loses the map, and the rescue never fires into a blow that would kill him. |
| **Mastery: Soulcairn call ↔ Wavelength** | One element at a time: a Wavelength element replaces the fallen element until its count is spent, then the fallen element returns if the call still runs. Only the Wavelength element spreads to adjacent enemies (*Core Rules → 11.5–11.6*). The Soulcairn partner is still no donor (*Open decisions → 25*). |
| **[Magic System](Magic-System.md)** | The system Wavelength reaches into, and the only one the Nexus touches outside itself. It is the **single written exception** to *Wie Elemente appliziert werden*: a non-mage applies elements through Combat Arts, and Dardan – with Wavelength active, for a counted number of attacks – applies with an ordinary strike (*Core Rules → 8.6*; the exception is named on the Magic System's side too, so neither document can be read alone and be wrong). **He applies, he never triggers:** a Wavelength attack onto an already applied element deals damage and leaves the element lying, the *Normaler Angriff (Nicht-Magier) auf Element* row unchanged. Everything else the element does – weaknesses, terrain fields, what a reaction needs – is the Magic System's and is not restated here. Dardan gains no MP rules, no tome access and no caster status from it. **Under Nexus Mastery the exception stays Dardan's and widens twice** (*Core Rules → 11.5–11.6*): a Wavelength element also lands on every enemy adjacent to the target, and the *Soulcairn* call lets his ordinary attacks apply the heaviest fallen unit's element for its duration – both apply, neither triggers, and he still carries one element at a time. The Magic System names the call beside Wavelength. **Dawnbreak does not touch the Magic System at all**: it applies no element – neither Dardan's Dendro nor a tome's nor a Wavelength element – and triggers no reaction; an element lying on a struck enemy stays where it is (*Core Rules → 10.6*). |
| **[Combat Arts](Combat-Arts.md)** | Untouched. Wavelength changes what Dardan's **ordinary** attacks carry; an art does what the art says, including applying its own element and triggering a reaction as any non-mage's art does. The one edge the two systems leave between them – an art with no element of its own, used while Wavelength runs – is *Open decisions → 29* and is not decided here. |
| **Beast Summon** | A summoned beast **cannot be exchanged, linked, healed by Heartpulse or sealed**, and – having no affinity and therefore no element to lend – can never be a **Wavelength** donor either, and – holding no rank with Dardan or anyone – can never be **bound by Bloodoath**; a bound Bestiarius's beast is not bound with it, and its attacks neither echo nor are echoed; and – holding no rank – a beast is **never counted by Dawnbreak**, and the line passes through it without striking it (conservative; *Open decisions → 49*). It is not an ally in the Nexus' sense and not an enemy of Dardan's. Since a beast can never be bound, its death never meets Bloodoath's defeat condition, which agrees with [Beast Summon](Beast-Summon.md)'s rule that a beast's death never contributes to one. The Nexus side of what [Beast Summon](Beast-Summon.md#interaction-with-other-mechanics) already states. A **wild** beast is an enemy and can be sealed; having no abilities, it loses nothing but – in Ch 52 – its chain. |
| **Swap, Reposition, Draw Back, Mystic Pull, Ensnare, Hurl, Uplift, Interpose** (positional commands and abilities) | Exchange is the only one that goes through everything and reaches beyond adjacency by rank; the rest stay adjacent or range 2 and are blocked by what stands between. Exchange does not replace them – it is on one unit, on a cooldown, and costs that unit its mobility. Interpose (Lance Paladin) is the Enemy-Phase mirror: a trade of places before the blow, which Dardan never gets before Nexus Mastery. Mastery's Enemy-Phase Exchange differs from it in kind: it fires only on a blow that has landed and would kill, it reaches by rank through everything, and it is once per ally per map (*Core Rules → 11.1*). |
| **Pressure Point** (Martial Saint), **Chokehold** (Enforcer), **Palm of Stillness** (Divine Monk art) | The nearest neighbours of Earthbound, all narrower: Pressure Point removes arts and magic for one turn, on hit; Chokehold denies healing and staves by adjacency, passively; Palm of Stillness stops move, attack and abilities for one turn at Master-art MP. Earthbound is longer (N rounds), silences auras too, is Dardan's alone, and is the only thing that cuts the Dajjal's chain. None of the three reaches a boss's mastery for a whole window. The former Saboteur ability *Sabotage* – a range-2 disable of arts, magic and abilities – was the one real overlap and has been **replaced by *Disarray*** (see the next row); an Advanced class no longer holds a stronger disable than the Lord's seal. |
| **Disarray** (Saboteur) | No overlap with the Nexus. Disarray scatters the enemies around a target; Earthbound closes one enemy's band. The two are complementary on a boss: Disarray strips the bodyguards off him, Earthbound strips his mastery. Its overlaps lie elsewhere – with Shove, Knockback and Earthshaker – and are noted on its catalog row. |
| **Staves and healing** | Heartpulse is not a staff: it grants no weapon XP, is not gated by rank, is not affected by staff rules, and reaches units no staff could. It is limited to one use per chapter precisely so that it does not replace them. Blessing, Grace, Sanctify and every other *on healing* trigger are worded on the **healer's** staff heal and do not fire on Heartpulse. |
| **Miracle, Intercession, Sworn Shield, Pavise, Bulwark, shields** | Reductions and redirections that act on the *ally's* incoming damage resolve **first**; Lifeline splits what is left. Miracle then checks each unit's own HP separately – a Cleric with Miracle survives her remainder at 1 HP while Dardan takes his share in full. Intercession's redirection to a Priest removes the damage from the linked ally entirely, so nothing reaches Dardan. Sworn Shield on Dardan does not cover his Lifeline share – it is not an attack on him. |
| **Status effects** (backlog) | **Lifeline redirects them:** any status the linked ally would receive lands on Dardan instead, whole. The Status Effects system therefore needs one hook – "would receive a status" – that Lifeline can intercept before the status is applied. Poison redirected to Dardan ticks on Dardan under the normal [poison floor](../Balancing-Guide.md#5--poison). A sleeping Dardan cannot use the Nexus that turn, like any unit that cannot act – so a sleep arrow aimed at the linked ally can switch the whole kit off for a turn; the forecast shows it. Irrelevant to Exchange's target (a sleeping ally can be swapped). A status on a sealed enemy is unaffected by the seal. **Under Nexus Mastery Heartpulse cures** every status on every unit it reaches, Dardan's redirected ones included (*Core Rules → 11.3*); the Status Effects system decides what is a status, and whether buffs and shields count is *Open decisions → 54*. A Dardan who cannot act does not trigger the Enemy-Phase Exchange (*Open decisions → 52*). |
| **Sanctuary, Hexward, Aurelian Ward** and every status immunity | An immunity on the **ally** never matters under Lifeline – the status was never going to land on the ally. An immunity on **Dardan** makes the redirected status fizzle: it reaches neither unit (conservative; *Open decisions → 9*). An Arch Bishop standing by Dardan therefore protects everyone Dardan is linked to. |
| **Consecration, Cleansing Palm, Aurevian Rite, Stillness** and every status cure | Cure Dardan like any unit. A status the band moved onto him is his; lifting the line does not return it. |
| **Burning Fields, Juggernaut** and every damage that is neither an attack nor a status | Not split by Lifeline; stays with the ally (conservative; *Open decisions → 8*). |
| **Canto, Wind Walk** and every "move again" effect | Never after an Exchange – the swap ends Dardan's movement. Never after a Dawnbreak either, in the conservative reading: the strike is Dardan's action and nothing follows it (*Core Rules → 10.1*). A sealed enemy loses Canto with its other abilities (conservative; *Open decisions → 14*). |
| **Unbreakable** (Armored General) | "Cannot be pushed, pulled or displaced" – it is an ability, so a *sealed* Armored General has lost it; an unsealed one is an enemy and Exchange never targets enemies anyway. No interaction on the player's side: an allied Armored General can be exchanged, because the Exchange is a trade both parties consent to, not a displacement. |
| **Tripwire, Cast Net, Grapple, Entangle** and every hold | A held ally can still be exchanged (its condition is irrelevant). A held *Dardan* cannot use commands or actions the hold forbids – the hold's own rule decides. Earthbound is not a hold and stacks with any of them. |
| **Chain Attack** | Independent. Dardan may be one of the three after an Exchange put him adjacent to the target; the swap is a command, the chain attack is the action. **Bloodoath keeps out of the Chain Attack's work:** an echo strike **does not wear down a boss's shield** – breaking the shield and the three-unit follow-through stay the [Chain Attack](Chain-Attack.md)'s (*Core Rules → 9.4*). The Echo still fires on a bound unit's ordinary attacks against a Special enemy; it just leaves the shield bar where it was. What happens to a bound unit's strikes **inside** a Chain Attack – whether they echo, and whether a kill there refreshes – is *Open decisions → 38*; conservatively the chain is untouched by the oath: its strikes do not echo, and a kill inside it neither refreshes nor spends the refresh. **Dawnbreak keeps out of it too**, conservatively: its strikes **do not wear down a boss's shield**, although the Chain Attack's own table gives ordinary attacks a small reduction – a once-per-chapter strike that reaches every Special enemy in a row at once, with no roll, would otherwise be a shield opener on any map with more than one; and it is **not a strike inside a Chain Attack** – Dardan's part in a chain is his ordinary attack, spell or art, as for any unit. A Special enemy on the line is still struck and still takes the strike's damage; only the shield bar stays where it was (*Open decisions → 47*). |
| **Bloodoath ↔ Exchange** | Exchange works normally and is the kit's way to put a unit into or out of the position the oath needs. Both cost Dardan's turn, differently: Exchange is a command, Bloodoath an action, so **the swap must come before the oath** in the same turn. Swapping a bound unit on a later turn is moot – the oath ends at the start of Dardan's next Player Phase. Dardan's post-Exchange tile is where he stands for the Enemy Phase; he is not bound, but his own fall loses the map as always. |
| **Bloodoath ↔ Lifeline** | Lifeline works normally on a bound ally and is the kit's direct insurance for the oath: Dardan carries the Lifeline share of every attack on it, which can keep it standing – paid with Dardan's HP, with no floor. Placing it is a command, so on the oath's own turn it comes **before** the swear; a line placed on an earlier turn holds as always. The stake is spread, not removed: the bound unit's fall loses the map, and so does Dardan's. |
| **Bloodoath ↔ Heartpulse** | Both are actions, so never in the same turn. A Heartpulse on the turn before the oath is the way to swear with both units at full health; during the oath Dardan has no action left. Heartpulse on either bound unit is ordinary healing by the ordinary rule. |
| **Bloodoath ↔ Earthbound** | Both are actions, so never in the same turn: Dardan chooses between sealing the enemy that threatens the pair and binding the pair. A seal set on an earlier turn and still running keeps working – a sealed enemy that attacks a bound unit is still attacked back and echoed. |
| **Bloodoath ↔ Soulcairn** | The Soulcairn partner is never bound. A bound unit that falls while the oath holds loses the map – in Classic the map resets, so that death never becomes permanent and **never enters Soulcairn's pool** (*Core Rules → 7, 9*). |
| **Bloodoath ↔ Wavelength** | Independent: Dardan is never bound, so his Wavelength strikes are never echoed and never trigger an Echo. Wavelength is a command and can precede the oath in the same turn; the element then rides Dardan's counters on the Enemy Phase as usual. A Wavelength donor may also be one of the bound – lending an element costs the donor nothing. |
| **Dawnbreak ↔ Exchange** | Exchange is a command and may precede the strike in the same turn: it is the kit's way to put Dardan at the head of a row, through walls, and his new tile is where the line starts and where the count is taken. The swapped ally stands on Dardan's old tile afterwards and is counted from there if that is within the bonus range. Exchange's cooldown and Dawnbreak's chapter use are independent. |
| **Dawnbreak ↔ Lifeline** | Independent. Dawnbreak deals no damage to allies and draws no counter, so no share is carried on the strike's turn by the strike itself. A linked ally near Dardan is counted like any other. |
| **Dawnbreak ↔ Heartpulse** | Both are actions and both are once per chapter, counted separately: using one never spends the other, and they are never used in the same turn. Heartpulse reaches every ranked unit on the map, Dawnbreak counts only those near Dardan – the same condition (a rank from D) read as width in two different radii. |
| **Dawnbreak ↔ Earthbound** | Both are actions, so never in the same turn. A sealed enemy on the line is struck like any other – the seal removes what runs through the band, not the enemy's body. Earthbound's cooldown and Dawnbreak's use are independent. |
| **Dawnbreak ↔ Soulcairn** | Soulcairn is **never counted**: it has no tile, and the line's length is the living who stand with Dardan. In Dardan's framing the cairn is already in the line – it is the base, what he carries when nobody stands with him; that is flavour, not a rule, and nothing in the base changes with the pool. The two meet in one situation only: a Dardan with no living ranked partner near him strikes with the base alone, and that is the situation in which Soulcairn's bonus is the *Affinity Bonus* term of his damage. Because Dawnbreak's strike uses the damage formula unchanged (conservative; *Open decisions → 41*), the cairn's bonus is in that strike too – the short line of a lone Dardan is the one strike the fallen still add to. |
| **Dawnbreak ↔ Wavelength** | Wavelength is a command and may precede the strike, but **Dawnbreak carries no element**: the borrowed element is not applied on the line and **no Wavelength attack is spent** by it. The loan stays on Dardan's blade for his ordinary attacks and counters. A donor near Dardan is counted like any ally. |
| **Dawnbreak ↔ Bloodoath** | Both are actions, so never in the same turn, and the oath ends at the start of Dardan's next Player Phase – so a Dawnbreak never falls inside an oath Dardan himself swore. Dardan is never bound, so his strike is never echoed and triggers no Echo, and a kill made with it never refreshes anyone. |
| **Piercing Shot** (Cannoneer), **Pierce** (Halberdier) | **No name collision.** *Piercing Shot* is the functional neighbour: a Cannoneer's Artillery attack also strikes every enemy on the straight line between it and its target. The two differ in what decides the line – Piercing Shot's is the weapon's range to the target, Dawnbreak's is the count of friends – and in how often: Piercing Shot is every attack of a Master class, Dawnbreak is once per chapter on one unit. Dawnbreak does not replace it. *Pierce* is a Defense-halving trigger and unrelated; the rules of Dawnbreak avoid the word "pierce" so that the two are never read together. |
| **Biorhythm** | Unrelated. The *Nexus* biorhythm type is a separate rule that happens to be Dardan's; nothing there reads the band's state, and nothing here reads resonance. |
| **Difficulty modes** (backlog) | No Nexus value is scaled by difficulty in this document. If a mode ever changes the cooldowns or the Lifeline share, the column belongs in the Balancing Guide's difficulty table, not here. |
| **Permadeath / Casual** (backlog) | Lifeline's floorlessness is the same in every mode; what Casual changes is what Dardan's fall means, not whether it happens. **Soulcairn exists in Classic only:** it is built from permanent losses, and in Casual there are none – a retreated unit is back next chapter with its bonds intact, so the pool is empty by construction and the ability is not shown. The Permadeath & Retreat specification inherits one requirement: a fallen unit's pair with Dardan keeps its points, frozen, and Soulcairn reads them (*Core Rules → 7*). Two edges are that specification's to settle and are listed under *Open decisions → 23–24*. **Wavelength is not mode-gated** and works in Casual like everywhere else; what a mode changes is only what happens when the donor goes down, which is *Open decisions → 26* – conservatively, the element Dardan already carries runs out its remaining attacks. **Bloodoath is not mode-gated either** and its defeat condition applies in both modes; in Classic the lost map resets, so a bound unit's fall under the oath is never a permanent loss. That the map resets on a defeat in Classic is taken as given by this rule and is not yet written in any specification – Permadeath & Retreat inherits it (*Open decisions → 40*). **Dawnbreak is not mode-gated** and works the same in both; in Classic a fallen unit is simply no longer there to be counted. |
| **Victory & Defeat Conditions** (backlog) | **Bloodoath is the first mechanic that adds a defeat condition during a battle** (*Core Rules → 9.6*): either bound unit's fall loses the map, from the swear until Dardan's next Player Phase. Level documents set their defeat conditions before the first turn; this one is created by a player action and ends at a stated moment. The backlog specification inherits the requirement – such a condition must be addable mid-map, must end on its own, and must be shown from the moment it exists – and is not specified here (*Open decisions → 40*). |
| **Part 05 / Part 06** | Dardan is absent from Hasan's strand, so Soulcairn – like the rest of the kit – does not exist on Levels 33B–40B. A unit lost there enters Dardan's pool at the reunion in Ch 41, when the chart's halves rejoin (*Core Rules → 7*). |
| **Deployment** | Nexus abilities reach only deployed units; a unit in the convoy has no tile to swap or heal, and no tile from which to be counted by Dawnbreak. |
| **Level 52 – the Dajjal** | Sealed for the duration = chain cut for the duration. Everything else is `levelcraft`'s (*Core Rules → 6*). **Flag:** the level must be designed for the Mastery kit – the seal that spreads to every enemy adjacent to its target, and the automatic Enemy-Phase Exchange (*Core Rules → 11.1, 11.4*). |

## UI & Display

- **On Dardan, always:** the band as an icon in its current colour – golden or black – from the first turn of the map; the *Exchange*, *Earthbound*, *Wavelength* and *Bloodoath* cooldown counters; the *Heartpulse* use and the *Dawnbreak* use (each available / spent). A black band greys every Nexus command and says why.
- **Exchange and Lifeline targeting:** because the range differs per ally, the UI highlights **units**, not tiles – every ally Dardan can reach is lit; every ally he cannot is shown with the reason ("rank too low for this distance", "landing tile not passable for Dardan", "landing tile not passable for the ally"). The two landing tiles are previewed before confirmation.
- **After an Exchange:** Dardan's movement is shown as spent; his remaining options (attack, item) stay lit.
- **Lifeline, while active:** a visible line between Dardan and the linked ally, on both units, at any distance. **In every battle forecast** involving the linked ally the damage is shown split: "ally takes X · Dardan takes Y", with Dardan's resulting HP – including a red state when Y would kill him – and any status the attack carries shown **on Dardan's side** of the forecast, since that is where it will land. No Dardan dies to a share the player did not see, and no Dardan falls asleep to an arrow the forecast showed hitting someone else.
- **Heartpulse preview:** before confirmation, every unit that will be healed is shown with its amount, so the player sees the rank-shape of the pulse and can decide whether this is the turn.
- **Earthbound preview:** the target's abilities, arts, tomes, staves and auras that the seal will remove are listed before confirmation; a target that would lose nothing shows "nothing to seal". **On a sealed enemy:** a seal icon with the rounds remaining; its greyed abilities on inspection; its auras no longer drawn on the map.
- **Locked chapter:** the black band is visible in the deployment screen, before the first turn, so that the player plans the map without the Nexus rather than discovering the lock on turn one.
- **Wavelength, before the command is confirmed:** the reachable allies are highlighted as **units**, as for Exchange and Lifeline, each labelled with **the element it lends** (the Magic System's own icon, so no second symbol is learned) and **how many attacks that pair's rank buys**; an ally out of rank-range is shown with the reason. A donor the player cannot use – no rank, a summoned beast, the Soulcairn partner – is shown greyed with the reason rather than hidden, so that the rule is legible from the screen.
- **Wavelength, while it runs:** the carried element and the **attacks remaining** sit on Dardan's unit display, and the element is drawn on his weapon so the state is visible on the map, not only in a menu. **In every battle forecast** the line says which element the strike will apply – and, when the target already carries an element, that **no reaction will be triggered**. That last line is the one thing the player could otherwise expect wrongly, and *No Hidden Information* means it is said before the attack, not after.
- **Bloodoath, before it is confirmed:** the allies Dardan can reach are highlighted as **units**, as for Exchange; after the first is chosen, the valid second units are lit – each labelled with **the pair's rank and its Echo range** – and every unit that cannot be the second is greyed with the reason ("out of Dardan's range", "no rank with the first", "summoned beast"). The confirmation screen states the **defeat condition in words** – "If *X* or *Y* falls before your next turn, the battle is lost" – and shows the enemies that can reach either of the two on the coming Enemy Phase, drawn from the ordinary danger overlay. The oath cannot be lifted, so this screen is the last moment the player can change his mind, and it says so.
- **Bloodoath, while it holds:** the two are **marked as bound** – a line between them, on both units, at any distance, visibly different from Lifeline's line so that the two are never confused – with the pair's Echo range drawn around each of them when either is selected, so that "is my partner in range" is answered on the map before a move is committed. The **objective display** carries the added defeat condition under the level's own, with the moment it ends ("until the start of your next turn"). The **act-again** state sits on both units: available, or spent, with the name of the unit that spent it.
- **Bloodoath, in every battle forecast** that involves a bound unit – attacking on the Player Phase, or its counter as previewed in the enemy's forecast and the danger overlay – an *Echo* line: either "*Y* strikes too", with the echo strike's own damage, hit and critical, "cannot be countered" and "no shield damage" on a Special enemy; or "**No Echo**" with the reason – "*Y* out of range (distance 5, pair range 3)", "*Y* has no weapon equipped", "*Y* cannot attack (asleep)", "*Y* lacks MP", or "target will fall before the Echo". When the attack would be the oath's first Player-Phase kill, the line adds "*X* acts again" – or "*Y* acts again" when the forecast shows the echo strike landing the killing blow. Every forecast in which a bound unit could fall shows that the **battle** would be lost, not only the unit, in the same red state Lifeline uses for Dardan.
- **Dawnbreak, the count, before anything is chosen:** when Dardan selects *Dawnbreak*, the bonus range is drawn around his tile and every **counted** ally inside it is marked, with the total – "*N* stand with you – line *L*" – in words and numbers. Every unit inside the radius that is **not** counted is greyed with the reason ("no rank with Dardan", "summoned beast", "not of your army"), so that the rule is legible from the screen and nobody is left wondering why the friend beside Dardan added nothing. The count is taken here and does not change before confirmation (*Open decisions → 43*).
- **Dawnbreak, the line, before it is confirmed:** each offered direction is drawn as a line of that length from the tile next to Dardan; a direction whose line holds no enemy is not offered. Where a wall cuts the line short, the line ends visibly at it, with the tiles it would have reached shown faded and the reason ("stopped by wall"). Every enemy on the line is marked with the damage it will take; enemies that will fall are marked as falling; allies, beasts and Other-faction units on the line are marked as **passed through, unharmed**. The forecast states "no counter-attack", and – because the name leads some players to expect *Lux* – "**no element, no reaction**" whenever an enemy on the line already carries an element. No hit or critical rate is shown because none is rolled (*Open decisions → 44*).
- **Dawnbreak, after it is used:** the use on Dardan shows *spent* for the rest of the chapter; nothing else persists on the map.
- **Soulcairn, on Dardan's affinity list:** its own row, named *Soulcairn*, with rank (letter and colour), points and element – the element being the heaviest fallen unit's, with that unit's name beside it. Placed **after every living partner** on the list, so that the tie-break order of [Affinity → 4](Affinity.md#4--the-combat-bonus) reads it last, which matches the rule that it never wins against a living partner. Not shown while the pool is empty; never shown in Casual.
- **Soulcairn, on the Affinity Chart:** the fallen keep their nodes and their greyed links ([Affinity → UI](Affinity.md#ui--display)); on Dardan's side those greyed links **visibly run into one node named *Soulcairn***, drawn in the rank colour of the pool. Selecting the node shows the pool's points, its rank, its element and which fallen unit carries it, and the frozen points of every fallen pair that feeds it – the same information as any pair, so that "why is it a B" is answered without a second screen.
- **Soulcairn, in the battle forecast:** when it applies – Dardan in combat, no living ranked partner within the bonus range – the *Affinity* line reads *Soulcairn* with the **name of the heaviest fallen unit**, the pool's rank colour and the bonus per type actually applied. When a living partner is in range the line names the living partner, as it always did, and Soulcairn is not mentioned. Nothing is ever shown that is not being applied.
- **Nexus Mastery, held:** a Mastery mark on the band icon, so that the player knows which rules apply. Every range highlight, preview and radius drawn by the kit already includes the range bonus where an upgrade names it – the player never adds it himself.
- **Mastery – the Enemy-Phase Exchange:** every allied unit carries a small **rescue marker** – *available* or *spent* – for the map, visible on the unit and in the unit list. On Dardan's Player Phase, the allies inside his Mastery Exchange range whose rescue is still available are marked on the map, so the player sees whom the band covers before ending the turn. In the enemy's battle forecast and on inspection of an enemy's threat, an attack that would kill such an ally shows "**Dardan takes this blow**" with the recalculated damage on Dardan and his resulting HP – or "**no rescue**" with the reason ("out of range", "rescue spent", "would kill Dardan", "tile not passable", "Dardan cannot act"). When it fires, the swap is shown on the Enemy Phase and the ally's marker turns to *spent*.
- **Mastery – two Lifelines:** two lines, each drawn between Dardan and its ally; every forecast that involves either linked ally shows that line's share. When both allies could be struck in the same Enemy Phase, Dardan's HP bar shows both potential shares, so the combined risk is read before the turn ends.
- **Mastery – Heartpulse preview:** besides each unit's amount, the statuses the pulse will remove are listed per unit.
- **Mastery – Earthbound preview:** the target and every adjacent enemy the seal will reach are marked, each with what it will lose; on the map each carries its own seal icon and rounds remaining.
- **Mastery – Soulcairn call:** the command appears on Dardan only when the pool is not empty, with the fallen element and the rank it would give. While it runs, a **counter of turns remaining** sits on Dardan beside the band icon, together with the carried fallen element; the battle forecast's *Affinity* line names whichever of Soulcairn and the best living partner is applied and why ("stronger"), and the element line says which element the strike applies – the fallen one, or the Wavelength element that replaces it. When the call is spent, it shows *spent* for the map.
- **Mastery – Wavelength:** the forecast marks every enemy adjacent to the target that the element will also land on, and those that already carry an element are shown as keeping it.
- **Mastery – Bloodoath:** the act-again state sits on each bound unit separately – available or spent – instead of one state shared by the pair.
- **Mastery – Dawnbreak:** the counting radius drawn around Dardan is the widened one, and the damage shown per enemy already includes the multiplier once it is set.

Items 27, 37 and 45 were answered by this decision and are kept below as closed stubs so that their numbers stay stable; item 6 is answered for the Mastery state.

1. **Which chapters lock the band – a pending story revision, not a spec gap.** The rule is fixed: a chapter locks the Nexus in its level box, and the band shows black. Which chapters do is Dardan's, and he has said the chapters were written before the band had a state and will be revised. What the story shows today, as the note for that revision: the band greys at the end of Ch 40A, is black in Ch 41–43, and is golden again from Ch 44 after the trial; Ch 30's box says "Exchange funktioniert in diesem Chapter nicht" while the prose describes the band as golden and refusing – whether that is a black chapter, an Exchange-only lock or a third thing is part of the same revision.
2. **A unit with no affinity rank is reached by nothing** – not exchanged, not linked, and **not healed by Heartpulse**: the *(no rank)* row of the Heartpulse table is zero. Answered by [Affinity](Affinity.md#8--what-nexus-reads) rather than by Dardan: the alternative – a lowest-row heal for strangers – would make Heartpulse the one Nexus ability that ignores the bond it is named for, and it would create a bond from nothing, which no other source does. Kept in the list so that Dardan sees it was decided on his behalf.
3. **Exchange once per turn** even with Nexus Mastery. Since the 2026-09-24 rework Mastery keeps the Exchange cooldown, so the rule only bites if the cooldown is ever tuned below one turn; it stays written so that no tuning can turn the command into a relay through the whole line in one Player Phase. The Enemy-Phase trigger is not a command and is counted apart (*Core Rules → 11.1*).
4. **Nexus Mastery's trigger.** It unlocks in **Part 08** (Ch 49–52, Lv 54–60) – that much is decided. Whether it comes at a chapter (the first Tower battle, the Dajjal's) or at a level is open.
5. **Lifeline rounding:** Dardan's share rounded down, remainder on the ally. Any rule works; this one favours Dardan by at most one point.
6. **One Lifeline at a time.** The alternative – several lines – multiplies the risk in a way the forecast can still show, but makes "whose burden" a list instead of a choice. **Answered for the Mastery state (Dardan, 2026-09-24): two at once, on two different allies** (*Core Rules → 11.2*). Before Mastery the conservative one-at-a-time stands.
7. **Lifeline holds at any distance once placed;** rank-range is checked only when it is set. The alternative – the line breaks when the two move out of rank-range – adds a state the player must track every move.
8. **Damage that is neither an attack nor a status stays with the ally** – Burning Fields, Juggernaut's passage. The alternative is that every HP loss the ally suffers is split; the line drawn here keeps "attack" and "status" as the two things the band carries.
9. **A status Dardan is immune to fizzles** – it reaches neither unit. The alternative is that an immune Dardan lets it fall back on the ally, which would make Sanctuary on Dardan worse for the ally than no Sanctuary at all.
10. **Applied elements are not status effects and stay on the ally.** The Magic System keeps the two apart (Cleansing Palm removes "every applied element and status effect"); if Dardan decides an applied Umbra or Cryo is an affliction the band carries, it moves to him like a status.
11. **Lifeline has no cooldown and lifting is free** (*confirm*). Its cost is Dardan's HP, his condition and the command. The alternative is a cooldown on placing it.
12. **Heartpulse heals per unit by that unit's rank**, not by Dardan's average rank (*confirm* – the reading of Ch 21's "jeder führte zu einem Paar").
13. **Heartpulse is a full action and includes Dardan** at a value of its own in the table. The alternatives: a command that leaves his action; Dardan excluded.
14. **Earthbound removes every ability**, flat stat passives and Canto included. The alternative is a narrower list – arts, magic, auras and triggered abilities, leaving +2 Speed and Canto in place – which is a different reading of "everything that runs through the Nexus".
15. **Nexus abilities take no Capacity and cannot be unequipped.** Forced by Ch 08 (Base class, Capacity 3); listed so it is a decision and not an accident.
16. **Lifeline's action cost is a command like Exchange – Dardan may still attack afterwards** (*confirm*).
17. **Lifeline's introduction chapter** – not decided; Dardan is still deciding. Until set, the ability is a proposal in `mechanicraft`'s sense.
18. **Earthbound cooldown versus duration.** If the cooldown is at least the duration, at most one enemy is sealed at a time; if shorter, two can be. Design intent proposed: one at a time. The values are the Balancing Guide's; the relation is Dardan's. Not moot in Part 08: Mastery keeps the cooldown (2026-09-24), but one seal there can close on several enemies at once – the target and its neighbours (*Core Rules → 11.4*) – so the relation decides whether two such clusters can be sealed at the same time.
19. **Seal immunity.** Nothing here makes any enemy immune to Earthbound, the Dajjal included. Whether Level 52 gates the seal behind a condition is the level's.
20. **The form of Soulcairn's in-world introduction at the first death – a proposal for Dardan, not a decision.** That it has no chapter gate and no tutorial map is decided (*Core Rules → 7*, *Introduction*); *how* the player first meets it is not. Three forms were on the table: a short map talk or a line from Dardan when the band first carries a dead bond; the chart entry alone, announced in the between-chapter selection; or nothing but the new row on Dardan's affinity list. **Proposed here, in the most conservative form: nothing but the new row** – the row and the new node appear, the forecast names the fallen unit the first time the bonus applies, and the player finds the rule by reading it, as he finds the *Affinity* line in Ch 06. Reason for the proposal, not for the decision: a scripted line would have to be written for every unit that can die in every chapter that can kill one, and a popup at the worst moment of a Classic run is the one place this game should not put a tutorial. If Dardan wants the moment spoken, the text is Lorekeeper's and the trigger is a story decision, not a rule this document may make.
21. **A new Soulcairn rank applies from the next map on, not on the map the death happened on.** The pool, its rank and its element are re-read the moment a unit falls (*Core Rules → 7.6*), but the **rank the bonus is paid at** follows the rule every other pair already obeys – the rank a pair holds when the map begins ([Affinity → Core Rules → 1](Affinity.md#1--the-pair-and-the-rank)): a bond deepens between the fights, not inside one. Conservative because it is the existing rule applied unchanged, and because the alternative – the cairn growing mid-battle – would make a death a same-map power spike, which is the one shape *Core Rules → 7* is built to avoid. Consequence to be aware of: on the map of the **first** loss the row exists but pays nothing, and the first bonus is drawn in the next chapter. Dardan's to widen if he wants the band to answer immediately.
22. **A black band switches Soulcairn off with the rest of the kit.** A chapter whose level box locks the Nexus locks the whole kit: no bonus from the cairn on that map, and the row is greyed like every other Nexus display (*Prerequisites*). Conservative because the lock is worded as the band being closed, and Soulcairn is the band carrying something; the alternative – a passive the lock cannot reach, since it costs nothing and needs no reach – is defensible and is Dardan's. The question only ever matters in a chapter that is both black and after a loss.
23. **What counts as a fall, beyond a unit defeated in battle.** *Core Rules → 7* reads the pool from units lost permanently in Classic. Whether a unit the **story** removes from the roster – written out, taken, killed in a chapter rather than on a map – enters the pool is **not decided here**, and this document does not decide it: it depends on what Permadeath & Retreat (backlog) calls a loss and on whether such a removal freezes the pair at all. Flagged rather than answered, because either reading is a statement – the band keeping a friend the story took, or the cairn holding only those the player lost himself.
24. **A mode change mid-campaign.** If Permadeath & Retreat ever allows a run to move between Classic and Casual, what happens to a pool built in Classic is that specification's to settle. Nothing here depends on the answer; it is listed so that the question is not discovered later inside this document.
25. **Whether the fallen lend their element to Wavelength – Dardan's, named as an idea and never decided.** **The rule standing is the conservative one: Wavelength reaches living allies only, and the *Soulcairn* partner is not a valid donor** (*Core Rules → 8.2*). *For it:* the dead are already a partner on Dardan's list with a rank and an element, so lending it would cost no new machinery, and it would mean the friend who is gone can still put something in his hand – with the appeal that it needs no range, since the cairn is always in range. *Against it:* every other rule of this kit keeps the fallen strictly weaker than the living, and a donor without a range requirement would be the **best** donor on most maps – no position to buy, no ally to keep alive in rank-range – which turns Soulcairn from a consolation into an upgrade and reopens exactly the question *Core Rules → 7* was built to close. If Dardan wants it, the honest shapes are a shorter attack count or the same range requirement the living have; both are his call, and neither is written here.
26. **What happens to the borrowed element if the donor falls during the same combat.** **Conservative rule standing: the element Dardan already carries runs out its remaining attacks** – the loan was made, the band carried it, and a count that vanishes mid-Enemy-Phase is a state the player cannot plan around. The alternative is that it **ends at once**, which reads the element as something the living donor sustains and makes protecting the donor part of the cost. Both are defensible and the choice is Dardan's; nothing else in the document depends on it.
27. **Whether Nexus Mastery touches Wavelength.** *Decided by Dardan, 2026-09-24 – closed.* Yes, by its own upgrade: the element lands on the target and every adjacent enemy, and the donor range gains the Mastery range bonus; the cooldown stays (*Core Rules → 11.6*).
28. **Whether Wavelength has its own range row or shares the band's.** Conservative: it uses the **same rank-range as Exchange and Lifeline** – the Balancing Guide's own reason for Lifeline applies unchanged ("the band reaches as far as the bond, whatever runs along it – one table to learn"). The alternative is a third table, which is one more thing to learn for a smaller ability. Tuning, not a rule, so the answer lives in the Balancing Guide; flagged here because no row has been written for it yet.
29. **A Combat Art with no element of its own, used while Wavelength runs.** Not decided. Does it carry the borrowed element – and therefore trigger a reaction, since an art triggers – or does Wavelength stay on ordinary attacks only? The rules above say only what is decided: Wavelength changes ordinary attacks, Combat Arts do what the art says (*Core Rules → 8.7*, *Interaction*). The question matters because the wider reading would hand Dardan the trigger that rule 7 denies him, through the back door of an elementless art. Dardan's, and it should be answered together with Combat Arts.
30. **Which attacks spend from the count.** Conservative: **an attack that lands spends one; an attack that misses applies nothing and spends nothing**, and counter-attacks on the Enemy Phase spend from the same count as Player-Phase strikes (*Core Rules → 8.4*). The alternatives – every swing spends, hit or miss, or Player Phase only – change how long a loan really lasts, so the answer belongs with the attack-count row in the Balancing Guide and is Dardan's.
31. **Wavelength's introduction chapter** – not decided, and **no chapter is proposed here**. Until Dardan sets one, the ability has no map, no level box and no entry in any level's *New Mechanics* column, and it is a proposal in `mechanicraft`'s sense, exactly like Lifeline (*→ 17*). What the introducing level will need is written in *Introduction* so that the choice is informed, not made.
32. **Whether Bloodoath's range from Dardan uses the band's shared table or a row of its own.** Conservative: the **same rank-range as Exchange and Lifeline**, for the reason the Balancing Guide already gives for Lifeline – one table to learn – and in the same state as Wavelength's (*→ 28*). The pair's **Echo range** is a separate row in any case: it reads a different rank. Tuning, not a rule; the answer lives in the Balancing Guide.
33. **When the Echo fires, and how often.** Conservative: **once per combat** in which a bound unit attacks an enemy – on the Player Phase or with a counter-attack on the Enemy Phase, with a weapon, tome or combat art – **resolved after that combat is over**, if the target still stands. The alternatives – an echo after every one of the attacker's strikes (so a doubling attacker brings two), or an echo inside the combat, before the enemy's counter – change both the strength and whether the echo can land a kill that spares the attacker a counter. Dardan's reference, Dual Strike, fires inside the combat; that is the widest reading and is his to choose.
34. **What an echo strike is – its strength, its weapon and what it carries.** Not decided; the strength row in the Balancing Guide is empty. Conservative: **one ordinary strike of the partner's own equipped weapon**, at full strength, with the ordinary hit and critical roll and the cost an ordinary attack of that weapon has (MP for a mage, a use where a weapon has uses); never a combat art; a unit with nothing equipped, a staff only or too little MP cannot echo. What that leaves open inside the conservative form, and which Dardan should answer with it: whether a mage's echo strike **applies its element and can trigger a reaction** as its ordinary attack would (the rules above do not forbid it, because forbidding it would be a second exception in the Magic System's application rules, beside Wavelength's; if it does, the reaction still wears no shield), whether abilities that trigger "on attack" fire on it, and whether it earns XP and weapon XP. The alternatives on strength – a fraction of an ordinary strike, or a fixed strike independent of weapon – are tuning and go into the empty row.
35. **What "act again" lets the refreshed unit do.** Conservative, following Dardan's Galeforce reference: the unit is refreshed as if it had not yet moved this turn – **full movement and a new action**. The alternatives – the action only, from where it stands, or the unit's remaining movement plus an action – are narrower. The wording should be confirmed by Dardan: he named the reference, not the extent of the refresh.
36. **Whether Bloodoath or its echo strikes are an affinity source.** Conservative: **swearing earns nothing**, and the oath adds no source of its own; what the two do under it counts only by Affinity's existing rules. Those rules already pay a **shared kill** when one damages a target and the other defeats it within the round, and an echo strike is an attack of the partner's – so a bound pair will naturally earn shared kills with each other, at the ordinary value and under the ordinary throttle. Whether the oath should pay more (a Nexus source like Lifeline's, for Dardan and each of the two, or a pair source for the two), or should suppress the shared kills it makes easy, is Dardan's; the Balancing Guide's *Points per source* table has no row for it.
37. **Whether Nexus Mastery touches Bloodoath.** *Decided by Dardan, 2026-09-24 – closed.* Yes, by its own upgrade: act-again once per unit instead of once per oath, and the Mastery range bonus on both ranges; the cooldown stays (*Core Rules → 11.7*).
38. **Bloodoath inside a Chain Attack.** Not decided. Conservative: **the Chain Attack is untouched by the oath** – a bound unit's strikes inside a chain do not echo, and a kill inside a chain neither refreshes nor spends the act-again. The alternative, that a chain strike echoes, would add a fourth attacker to a formation the Chain Attack defines as three; that a chain kill refreshes would hand a second turn to the unit that finished a boss's shield phase. Both belong with the Chain Attack's own revision.
39. **Bloodoath's introduction chapter** – not decided, and **no chapter is proposed here**. Until Dardan sets one, the ability has no map, no level box and no entry in any level's *New Mechanics* column, and it is a proposal in `mechanicraft`'s sense, exactly like Lifeline (*→ 17*) and Wavelength (*→ 31*). What the introducing level will need, including that it must not force the oath, is written in *Introduction*.
40. **What the defeat condition rests on – two backlog systems, not specified here.** The oath's risk is written as "the map is lost, as it is when Dardan falls; in Classic the map resets". Neither half is yet in any specification: Dardan's fall as a defeat condition and what a lost map does belong to **Victory & Defeat Conditions**, the Classic reset to **Permadeath & Retreat**, both on the [mechanics backlog](README.md#backlog--not-yet-documented). Bloodoath adds the first requirement Victory & Defeat Conditions has to meet – a defeat condition added by a player action mid-map, ending at a stated moment, shown from the moment it exists – and nothing in this document decides how that system is built.
41. **What a Dawnbreak strike is – its strength and what it carries.** Not decided; the strength row in the Balancing Guide is empty. Conservative: against each enemy on the line, **one ordinary strike of Dardan's equipped weapon**, computed by the [damage formula](../Balancing-Guide.md#-damage-calculation-formula) unchanged – the weapon's numbers and effectiveness, the triangle, the target's terrain and Def or Res, and the *Affinity Bonus* term as it stands (which is Soulcairn's when nobody living is near). One strike per target, no follow-up, never a combat art; Dardan must hold a weapon with which he could make an ordinary attack, or Dawnbreak is not offered. Left open inside that form, and to be answered with it: what the strike costs the weapon (one ordinary attack's cost, or one per target – Durability is on the backlog), whether it earns XP and weapon XP and how much, whether abilities that trigger "on attack" or "on kill" fire on it, and whether post-action movement (Canto and the like) may follow it – conservatively not. The alternatives on strength – a multiple or a fraction of the ordinary strike, or a fixed value independent of the weapon – are tuning and go into the empty row.
42. **Dawnbreak's directions.** Conservative: the **four grid directions** only. Diagonals would give eight lines instead of four and change what "a row of enemies" means on the grid – a diagonal line of eight tiles covers far more ground than a straight one – so they are Dardan's to add, not a default.
43. **When the length is counted, and what the preview shows.** Conservative: the count is taken **when Dawnbreak is chosen** and shown before it is confirmed – the line in each direction, the counted allies and the ones not counted with the reason, the wall that stops it and every enemy it strikes with the damage each takes; nothing during the strike changes it. Filled in with it, as a rule of the same conservative reading: **a direction whose line holds no enemy is not offered**, and Dawnbreak is not offered at all when no direction holds one – a once-per-chapter action should not be spendable on empty tiles. Dardan may prefer to allow an empty strike.
44. **No hit roll and no critical roll.** Chosen as the reading most consistent with [Pillar 5](../Design-Pillars.md) and the existing kit, **and flagged**, because the strike is a weapon strike: every enemy on the line takes the damage the preview shows. The kit's standing rule is that no Nexus ability rolls to hit (*Design Pillars* table, Fairness) and Earthbound was built on the same argument – a use that can whiff is a use lost to dice, and here the use is the chapter's only one. The alternative is the reading Bloodoath's echo strike follows: an ordinary strike with the ordinary hit and critical roll per target, each rate shown in the forecast. That reading keeps the strike an ordinary one and would make the enemies' Avoid and terrain matter to it; it is Dardan's to choose, and if he does, the Fairness row of the Pillars table has to be reworded.
45. **Whether Nexus Mastery touches Dawnbreak.** *Decided by Dardan, 2026-09-24 – closed.* Yes, by its own upgrade: a flat damage multiplier (its value is *→ 58*) and a counting radius of bonus range plus the Mastery range bonus; the cap, the once per chapter and the walls are unchanged (*Core Rules → 11.8*).
46. **Whether Dawnbreak is an affinity source.** Conservative: **no** – the strike earns nothing itself, and a kill made with it counts only as [Affinity](Affinity.md#2--earning-points-deeds-never-proximity) already counts a kill: an enemy an ally damaged earlier in the round and Dawnbreak finishes is an ordinary *shared kill* for that ally and Dardan. The alternative – a small source for Dardan with every counted ally, since they "stood with him" – would pay for **proximity**, which Affinity forbids by rule, and would make Dawnbreak the one Nexus ability that grows the count it reads. Dardan's to decide against that rule, not a tuning choice.
47. **Boss shields and the Chain Attack.** Conservative: Dawnbreak **does not wear down a boss's shield**, although the [Chain Attack](Chain-Attack.md)'s table gives ordinary attacks a small reduction; a strike that reaches every Special enemy in a row at once without a roll would otherwise be the fastest shield-opener on any map with more than one, and Bloodoath's echo strike keeps out of the shield for the same kind of reason. It is **not a strike inside a Chain Attack** either – Dardan's part in a chain is his ordinary attack, spell or art. The alternative – the ordinary-attack reduction on each Special enemy struck – belongs with the Chain Attack's own revision.
48. **Dawnbreak's introduction chapter** – not decided, and **no chapter is proposed here**. Until Dardan sets one, the ability has no map, no level box and no entry in any level's *New Mechanics* column, and it is a proposal in `mechanicraft`'s sense, like Lifeline (*→ 17*), Wavelength (*→ 31*) and Bloodoath (*→ 39*). What the introducing level will need – a row of enemies, ranked allies who can close in so the player watches the count grow, one wall that stops the line, and no pressure to fire it on turn one – is written in *Introduction*.
49. **What stops the line, and what it passes through.** Conservative: a **wall** is a tile no unit of any Move Type may enter – walls, closed doors, pillars, and whatever else the Movement rules (backlog) make impassable to fliers too – or a magical barrier; the line stops **before** it. An **open door** is floor. Terrain that only some Move Types cannot cross (water, chasms, cliffs) does not stop it, and an enemy flier over it is struck. **Elevation** is not a wall; no height rule exists yet, and the Movement specification should say whether a cliff face stops a line. The line **passes through**, unharmed, units of the player's faction, **Other-faction units and summoned beasts** (they are not Dardan's enemies), and any unit **hidden from the player** – so that the preview is exactly what is struck; a hidden enemy revealed before the strike (by *Revelation* or otherwise) is struck like any other. A **wild** beast is an enemy and is struck. Each of these lines is Dardan's to move; the Movement specification inherits the requirement to define "impassable to every Move Type".
50. **Nexus Mastery's Capacity in the catalog.** [Abilities → Lord Kit](../catalog/Abilities.md#lord-kit) lists *Nexus Mastery* at **Capacity 4**, while every Nexus ability takes no Capacity (*→ 15*) and Mastery is now nothing but their upgrade. The number is left as it stands; whether Mastery costs Capacity – and could therefore be unequipped, switching every upgrade off – is Dardan's. The reading consistent with *→ 15* would be none.
51. **What the Enemy-Phase Exchange reads, and how the blow ends.** Filled in conservatively where the decision was silent (*Core Rules → 11.1*): "would kill" is checked **after** every reduction and redirection the ally has, a Lifeline split included, and counts only deterministic effects – a survival ability that rolls (a Miracle-type chance) is not anticipated; the recalculated hit keeps the rolled hit and critical and reads Dardan's Def or Res, his new tile's terrain, and the triangle and effectiveness against his weapon and class; Dardan takes it whole, with no Lifeline share; "the attack ends" means no further strike of that combat and no counter by Dardan or the ally in it. Each line is Dardan's to move – most of all whether Dardan may counter after taking the blow.
52. **When the Enemy-Phase Exchange is spent, and when it cannot fire.** Conservative: the once-per-unit-per-map is spent **only when it fires** – a check that fails (out of range, a tile not passable, a blow that would kill Dardan) spends nothing. It does **not fire while Dardan cannot act** – asleep, paralysed or under any status that stops a unit acting – on the same reading that a sleeping Dardan cannot use the Nexus (*Interaction → Status effects*), and not on a black band. The alternative – an automatic rule that works on a sleeping Dardan, since nothing is chosen – is defensible and is Dardan's.
53. **Whether the Enemy-Phase Exchange is an affinity source.** Conservative: it **is an Exchange** and pays the existing *Nexus – Exchange* source ([Balancing Guide → Points per source](../Balancing-Guide.md#points-per-source)) exactly as the command does – no new row, no extra value. The alternative – that an automatic swap earns nothing, because Dardan chose nothing – is Dardan's.
54. **The cleansing Heartpulse – what it removes and whom it heals higher.** Filled in conservatively (*Core Rules → 11.3*): it removes what Lifeline would move – afflictions in the Status Effects system's sense; **applied elements** stay (*→ 10*) and staff **buffs and shields** stay; the cure is **not** a separate *Help – cure* affinity source – the pulse pays its Heartpulse row only. "One row higher": an **S** unit stays on the S row; **Dardan himself** stays on his own row, since he holds no rank with himself and the pulse goes outward. Each is Dardan's to widen – whether the pulse strips buffs, and whether Dardan is lifted one row as well.
55. **What "adjacent" means for the spreading seal and the spreading element.** Conservative: the **four grid neighbours** of the target's tile, at the moment the seal is set or the element applied – the same four directions Dawnbreak uses (*→ 42*). Diagonals would widen both upgrades to eight tiles. The seal's spread closes on enemies only and reaches no further than those neighbours; the element's spread lands on enemies only.
56. **"Only the stronger counts" during the Soulcairn call.** Conservative: the stronger is decided by the rule [Affinity → 4](Affinity.md#4--the-combat-bonus) already uses for several partners – the higher rank, then more points, then the order of the affinity list, on which Soulcairn stands last. The alternative – comparing the actual bonus values, which differ by element mix – would need a new comparison rule the player has to compute; the rank order is already on screen.
57. **The Soulcairn call's element – fixed, and not spreading.** Conservative: the heaviest fallen unit's element is read **at the moment of the call** and does not change during it, even if a heavier unit falls meanwhile – as Wavelength's is fixed at the command (*Core Rules → 8.5*). Its attacks apply it to the **target only**: the adjacent-enemy spread is Wavelength's upgrade (*Core Rules → 11.6*) and the call is not Wavelength. Both are Dardan's to widen.
58. **The Dawnbreak Mastery multiplier – value, and what happens until it is set.** The lever exists in the [Balancing Guide → Nexus Mastery](../Balancing-Guide.md#nexus-mastery) and is **not yet set**. Until it is, the Mastery strike is computed **without** it – the upgrade's damage half has no effect, and the strike is what *Core Rules → 10.6* and *→ 41* make it. Conservatively the multiplier applies to each target's final damage from the ordinary formula, rounded down; where it sits against the separate, also unset *strike strength* lever (*→ 41*) should be decided with both values.
